## 0. What the study established (one paragraph)

Extending Fable–Carson with a fourth regime — negotiated cession, expressed
in their own parameter vocabulary — and joining it against S16's rent side:
**ceding negotiated control of one's compute is already individually
rational wherever the hardware can participate in a market at all.** At
hardware-partitioned isolation (S16 Case 3/4) the residual risk term
collapses to ~$0.25/machine-yr — the observed market rent clears the
crossover by ~2.5× — so the inequality is decided by participation and
energy, not by security. The only configuration where sharing destroys value
is unnegotiated, unpartitioned access at pessimistic incident rates
(−$1,886/machine-yr) — which is the configuration we call malware. In the
model's own currency, a cession-regime world turns the AI cost delta
negative (defense-like median, but a fatter tail: one partition design on
every endpoint is Böhme–Kataria's same-production-batch cell — correlation
is the price of the architecture). And the horizon arms show the regime's
value is overwhelmingly avoided losses and converted defense spend, not the
rent income itself.

## 2. III.3 — the negotiated-cession continuation (new paragraphs)

Placement: end of III.3, where the body metaphor currently rests. This is
the study's answer to "what would the tolerated/symbiotic infection look
like as a contract."

> What does the body metaphor look like as a contract? We extended the
> Fable–Carson engine with a fourth regime and priced it: a world in which
> a material share of endpoints runs manufacturer-partitioned
> shared-execution domains — owner-untouchable, underwriter-insured — and
> access to them is sold under negotiated terms rather than taken by
> exploitation. The categories rewrite themselves in the arithmetic.
> Unauthorized becomes unnegotiated; compromise becomes breach of contract;
> ownership becomes what it already is in the economics of the firm —
> residual rights of control over a negotiated bundle (Grossman & Hart
> 1986; Hart & Moore 1990). The law is already moving: since 2022,
> good-faith security research is not charged under the CFAA as a matter of
> prosecutorial policy, and the vulnerability-reward programs have priced
> negotiated disclosure for a decade (Finifter, Akhawe & Wagner 2013).
>
> Joined against the idle-fleet economics, the striking result is where the
> decision actually binds. At hardware-partitioned isolation the expected
> residual loss from sharing one's machine is measured in cents per year;
> the observed market rent clears the break-even rate severalfold. Risk is
> not the constraint — participation is: the same join prices today's
> integrated-graphics business fleet at zero not because sharing it is
> dangerous but because no market will accept it. The one configuration
> that destroys value is unnegotiated access without isolation, at roughly
> minus one thousand nine hundred dollars per machine-year under
> pessimistic incident rates — malware, priced. The distance between the
> infection the body fights and the flora it hosts is, in this arithmetic,
> a partition boundary and a contract.
>
> The extension carries an honest counter-channel: a single partition
> design and a single negotiation protocol on the whole installed base is
> the same-faulty-production-batch cell of the cyber-insurance correlation
> literature (Böhme & Kataria 2006). In the model this shows up exactly
> where it should — the cession regime's median cost delta goes negative
> while its tail stays fatter than the defense-dominant regime's.
> Symbiosis thins the body of the loss distribution and concentrates what
> remains in correlated failures of the symbiont itself. That is V.2's
> pluralism argument arriving from the security side: the cure for the
> commons' monoculture is more than one commons.

*(Cites: Grossman-Hart 1986, Hart-Moore 1990, DOJ Justice Manual §9-48.000
(May 2022), Finifter et al. 2013, Böhme-Kataria 2006 — all verified.
Constraints honored: "prosecutorial policy," and negotiated-intrusion
framing is ours, not Finifter's. The $ figures trace to
`analysis/negotiated-cession/crossover.csv`.)*
