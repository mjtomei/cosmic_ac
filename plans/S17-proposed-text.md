## 0. The argument (Matthew, 2026-08-06, his words)

> It's about how if you could account for externalities, profit on non-rival
> goods is lower or even negative. And you use examples of power abusing the
> systems meant to protect people who originate things. How requiring
> participation in the economy for society to acknowledge your right to think
> creates those kinds of perverse incentives. And how machine intelligence
> which tracks what everyone does as well as being open is the solution for
> assigning credit and allocating resources that actually has the lowest cost
> when that intelligence is nearly free, which is what the trend seems to be
> moving towards.

Four legs. The S17 recompute survives as leg 1's trend coda, no longer the

## 1. The ledger: externalities accounted, non-rival profit is small or negative

Two readings of "negative," both now carrying verified anchors; which one
leads is Matthew's call (open question 1):

**(a) The originator's ledger.** The producer bears the full cost and
captures a sliver of the social product. Measured: an extra R&D dollar
returns ~21 cents/yr to the spender and 55–58 cents to the economy (Bloom-
Schankerman-Van Reenen, Econometrica 2013; the 2019 update widens it to
13.6% vs 57.7%, a 4:1 ratio); the aggregate accounting puts $5 per invested
dollar conservatively, $10–20 plausibly (Jones & Summers, NBER 27863);
Nordhaus's innovator kept 2.2% of the surplus. The uncompensated remainder
IS the positive externality, and every innovation whose private sliver falls
short of its cost is a donation the market told the originator not to make.

**(b) The exclusion regime's ledger.** The apparatus that makes non-rival
goods sellable has costs of its own, and counted against what it actually
delivers to originators it runs negative in the measured cases: outside
chemicals and pharmaceuticals, aggregate patent litigation costs (~$12B/yr,
1992 dollars) exceeded aggregate patent profits (~$3B/yr) for US public
firms by the late 1990s (Bessen & Meurer, *Patent Failure*, 2008 —
contested, cite with the Schwartz-Kesan flag); when exclusion pricing was
simulated onto Indian quinolones, consumer losses exceeded the patent
holder's gain by an order of magnitude (~$255M vs ~$19.6M/yr, Chaudhuri-
Goldberg-Jia, AER 2006 — name the scenario). Arrow stated the double loss at
the origin: underinvestment (p.619) AND "precisely to the extent that it is
successful, there is an underutilization of the information" (p.617). The
selling regime loses on both margins at once.

**The trend coda (the v1 study, demoted).** Whatever the level, the sale
channel is dying on its own terms: re-solving Nordhaus at measured AI
imitation lags takes capture from 2.2% to ~0.2% (11–15×), negative control
flat (`analysis/s17/README.md`). The wedge a compensation channel must carry
is 99.8% and widening.

**Candidate prose (leg 1):**

> Arrow wrote the ledger down in 1962. A free enterprise economy
> underinvests in invention because the product can be appropriated only to
> a limited extent, and where appropriation does succeed, he noted,
> "precisely to the extent that it is successful, there is an
> underutilization of the information." The selling regime loses coming and
> going. Sixty years of measurement filled in the magnitudes. An additional
> dollar of R&D returns about twenty-one cents a year to the firm that
> spends it and fifty-five to the economy around it. On the aggregate
> accounting, a dollar invested in innovation yields five dollars of social
> benefit under conservative assumptions and ten or twenty under plausible
> ones. The innovator's own take of the surplus, measured across the postwar
> economy, was 2.2 percent. Written as a balance sheet with the externality
> on it, origination is a donation, and the price system keeps advising
> against it.

*(Cites for the paragraph: Arrow 1962 pp.617/619; BSV Econometrica 2013;
Jones-Summers 2020; Nordhaus 2004. Register check against notes.txt before

## 2. Power abusing the protective systems

The protective institutions protect incumbents, and largely fail the
originators they were named for. Ordered from failure to abuse:

- **They barely protect**: patents raise imitation cost ~7% (Mansfield 1981;
  Levin et al. 1987: 7–15%), and rank last or near-last among appropriability
  mechanisms in most industries, behind secrecy and lead time (Cohen-Nelson-
  Walsh 2000). FRAMING CONSTRAINT: prevent-copying is still the most-cited
  reason to patent (95.8%); the defensible claim is that strategic motives
  (blocking 81.8%, negotiation, suit-prevention) are cited by large
  majorities and dominate among heavy patenters — "Mostly, your patents are
  used in horse trading" (CNW's executive quote).
- **They exclude at scale**: Humira, 73 US patents, "80% non-patentably
  distinct," ~20 years to a US biosimilar vs months in Canada/UK (Goode &
  Chao 2022); evergreening "maintains, not reduces" incumbent market life
  (Hemphill & Sampat 2012); the anticommons — fragmented upstream rights
  blocking use — named in Science in 1998 (Heller & Eisenberg).
- **They are litigated as a business**: NPE disputes cost defendants ~$29B in
  2011 (Bessen & Meurer, Cornell L Rev 2014) — contested (Schwartz & Kesan:
  partly transfers, sample self-selected); carry the dispute, it does not
  change the leg.
- **They are extended retroactively for holders, not creators**: the 1998
  copyright term extension added twenty years to existing works; seventeen
  economists, **Kenneth Arrow among them** with four other Nobel laureates,
  told the Supreme Court the extension was worth a 0.33% increase in
  present-value payments to a new work's author and nothing to incentives
  for existing works, while a deadweight loss today is "224 times as large
  in present value as a deadweight loss eighty years from now." They lost,
  7–2 (Eldred v. Ashcroft, 2003). The man who named the appropriability
  problem told the Court the protective system had been captured, and the
  Court kept it.
- **The system's own reviewer**: Machlup, Senate Study No. 15 (1958), p.80:
  "If we did not have a patent system, it would be irresponsible, on the
  basis of our present knowledge of its economic consequences, to recommend
  instituting one."
- **Capture needs no patents**: academic publishing — "publishers neither
  pay the provider of the primary good—authors of scholarly papers—nor for
  the quality control—peer review" (Larivière et al., PLoS ONE 2015; top
  five publishers >50% of papers), at a 38.4% segment operating margin
  (RELX 2024 annual report, computed from segment figures).

Register note: this leg is where the Arrow-clause machinery attaches —
extraction through captured protective institutions is "sustained extraction
without legitimacy," the pressure that discharges through the cheapest
channel. Say it once.

## 3. The perverse incentive at the root

Matthew's sentence, carried as close to verbatim as prose allows:
**requiring participation in the economy for society to acknowledge your
right to think creates those kinds of perverse incentives.** The
originator's menu under that requirement: sell exclusion (leg 2's games),
sell yourself to an institution that captures the output, or stop. Leg 2 is
downstream of this conditioning.

- **Arrow again, both halves**: optimal allocation needs financing "not
  governed by profit-and-loss criteria," and "the bulk of basic research has
  been carried on outside the industrial system" already (p.623); "there is
  really no need for the firm to be the fundamental unit of organization in
  invention... individual talents count for a good deal more than the firm"
  (p.624).
- **The existing counterexample society**: science. Merton 1957 — "property
  rights in science become whittled down to just this one: the recognition
  by others of the scientist's distinctive part in having brought the result
  into being"; Dasgupta & David 1994 — open Science and proprietary
  Technology as two reward systems over the same non-rival knowledge,
  priority-of-disclosure vs secrecy-for-rent (verbatim quote needs the
  full-text pull; paraphrase is safe). Science demonstrates that a society
  can acknowledge thought without requiring its sale. Its pathologies
  (citation gaming, priority races) are the honest preview of leg 4's
  Goodhart bound and belong in the same breath.
- **Repo tie-ins**: the economic face of thoughts-allowed (finished paper
  2.2.3); V.3's engineered-preferences fork; open-weights-as-bequest.

## 4. The open estimator: credit assignment as the lowest-cost institution

Arrow specified the requirement and rejected it on cost, in the same pages:
optimal appropriation would need "elaborate distinctions of partial property
rights of all degrees," and patent laws "would have to be unimaginably
complex and subtle to permit such appropriation on a large scale" — so real
patent law crudely restricts instead (pp.617–618). And he named the
alternative channel: an economy where "the reward for invention would be
completely separated from any charge to the users of the information"
(p.617 — filed under ideal socialism; the separation, not the planner, is
the design). The leg's claim: machine intelligence that observes
contributions is the "unimaginably complex and subtle" system becoming
buildable, and at nearly-free intelligence it becomes the cheapest
institution available — cheaper than exclusion plus litigation, which is
leg 1(b)'s negative ledger. **Framing correction (Matthew, 2026-08-06): "It's
not the price of measuring intelligence that is falling. It's the price of
intelligence itself. So that makes things like the impossibly complex
accounting possible."** Arrow rejected the elaborate accounting on cost of
running it; the falling input is general intelligence, and the accounting is
one of the things it buys. Never frame the trend as cheaper sensors or a
cheaper measuring apparatus.

- **The cost trend**: price to reach a fixed capability level falling 9× to
  900× per year depending on task (Epoch AI, Mar 2025; their caveat: the
  fastest drops are recent, persistence unclear). Do not use the "1000× in
  two years" variant.
- **The formalism and its hardness**: credit assignment in a commons is the
  Shapley problem — #P-complete even for games as simple as weighted
  majority games (Deng & Papadimitriou 1994), approximated in practice
  (Data Shapley, ICML 2019; IJCAI 2022 survey). The approximation is the
  attack surface; say so.
- **The precedent that already migrated sale → compensation**: performing
  rights. The Supreme Court's own account of ASCAP's 1914 founding:
  performances "so fleeting, that as a practical matter it was impossible
  for the many individual copyright owners to negotiate with and license the
  users and to detect unauthorized uses" (BMI v. CBS, 441 U.S. 1, 1979) — an
  institution founded on measurement cost. Radio-era allocation ran on a
  0.1% sample, "99.9% of all performances go undetected" (Massarsky 1993);
  ASCAP's standing rule is census when measurement is cheap enough, sample
  otherwise; streaming shifted distribution from sampling toward census as
  measurement cost collapsed. And the moment measurement was solved, the
  dispute moved to valuation — pro-rata vs user-centric (CNM/Deloitte 2021:
  top-10 artists −12 to −17%, classical +24%) — the elicitation bound
  arriving exactly on schedule.
- **Openness is the design constraint, not a preference**: who-runs-the-
  estimator (surveillance pricing vs financial aid — same mechanism,
  acceptance turns on governance and surplus destination; Samuelson notes
  Addendum 3) plus IV.4's aggregation-point constraint. "Tracks what
  everyone does" without openness is the extractive twin.
- **The three bounds carried from v1** (verified,
  `analysis/s17/externality_anchor_verification.md`): measurement does not
  produce valuation agreement (SCC $51→$190 with the not-only-discounting
  framing constraint); Goodhart at the sanction; estimator governance. None
  technological.

**Candidate prose (leg 4, opening move):**

> Arrow measured the impossibility himself. Proper payment for information
> would require "elaborate distinctions of partial property rights of all
> degrees," patent laws "unimaginably complex and subtle," and so the actual
> law restricts crudely in the interest of enforceability. The performing
> rights societies lived inside that constraint for a century: founded, as
> the Supreme Court later put it, because performances were too fleeting for
> owners to detect, they allocated royalties from a 0.1 percent sample of
> the airwaves. Streaming made the census affordable and the sampling ended.
> Intelligence itself is now getting cheaper by ninefold to nine-hundredfold
> a year at fixed capability. Arrow set the elaborate accounting aside
> because running it was unaffordable, and that is the constraint now moving.

*(Cites: Arrow 1962, BMI v. CBS 1979, Massarsky 1993, ASCAP rules, Epoch
2025. Closing sentences rewritten 2026-08-06 per Matthew's correction: the
falling price is intelligence itself, and the impossibly complex accounting
is what it makes possible.)*
