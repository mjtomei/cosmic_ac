# §4.5a — WITHDRAWN. The mirror test moves to future work.

**Status: this proposal is withdrawn. §4.5a stands as written.** Kept as a
record of why, because the reasoning matters more than the result.

## Why it was withdrawn (Matthew, 2026-08-13)

**1. The two instruments are not comparable, so one cannot overturn the other.**
Kobak's list is EXOGENOUS: derived from PubMed excess vocabulary, with no US or
UK legislature in view. That the United States scores high on it is a finding
about the US, arrived at afterwards. The mirror instrument is ENDOGENOUS: its
words were selected precisely because they separate US Congress from UK
Commons. Setting a constructed gap against a discovered one and calling the
difference a correction is a category error. The mirror test measures a
different quantity; it does not refute §4.5a.

**2. The reported asymmetry was the smallest of three defensible framings, and
it was chosen without saying so.** The same four series give:

| framing | asymmetry |
|---|---|
| percent of own base | 1.5x  <- what the withdrawn draft reported |
| OLS slope | 1.15x |
| absolute change per 100k | **4.4x** (UK +49.7, US +11.3) |

Percentage growth flatters the US because its base on British words is already
higher. The absolute difference is four times larger than the figure that was
used to argue the asymmetry was "modest". That the three disagree by a factor
of four is itself the result: the summary statistic is carrying the argument,
which is the condition under which a finding must not be load-bearing.

## What survives, and where it goes

The mirror construction is a good instrument for a question §4.5a does not ask,
and it belongs in §8.6 as one case of a general design:

> **Directional influence between legislatures.** §4.5a establishes that the
> instrument rate rises everywhere except the United States. It does not
> establish which way usage travels, because a single exogenous word list
> cannot: it registers movement toward its own orientation and is silent about
> movement along any other axis.
>
> The general form is to select, for an ordered pair of chambers and a
> pre-treatment window, the vocabulary that most distinguished each from the
> other, then track both lists in both corpora afterwards. Applied to US
> Congress and UK Commons on a 1994-96 base this is already runnable
> (`mirror_instrument.py`) and gives Britain adopting American vocabulary and
> America adopting British, with the asymmetry between 1.15x and 4.4x
> depending on whether slope, percentage or absolute change is the summary --
> a spread wide enough that the design needs a pre-registered statistic before
> it can carry a claim.
>
> The interesting version is not one pair. Influence need not originate in one
> place, and the same construction runs over any pair: Ireland against
> Westminster, the Canadian provinces against Ottawa, the Australian states
> against each other, Scotland and Wales against Westminster since devolution.
> That yields a directed graph of lexical influence rather than a single
> hegemon story, and it can test whether a chamber that leads on one axis
> follows on another. The reversion controls the pilot already implements --
> excluding the selection window and tracking each list in its own source --
> are what keep such a graph from being an elaborate picture of regression to
> the mean.

## The secularism test is a separate, and clean, negative

Unaffected by the above, because it uses no constructed instrument:
religious-reference density against the Kobak rate is **+0.041** across 22
chambers, where a secularism proxy predicts a strong negative; within countries
the signs disagree (Canada +0.42, Australia -0.14, UK+IE -0.60, none
significant) with religiosity varying 2.5-5x. The decisive case is that the
most religious chamber in the sample, US House at 113 refs/100k, is also near
the top of the instrument. `secularism_check.py`.

Worth a sentence in §4.5a or §9 as a ruled-out reading. The CONSTITUTIONAL
version of the hypothesis is untestable here -- constitutional secularism does
not vary within Canada or Australia, and between countries n is about five.
