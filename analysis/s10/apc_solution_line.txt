READING
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The cell returns a NEGATIVE age slope: within the pre-drift Senate,
  every extra decade of age costs about 0.9 per 1,000 of the register.
  That is the same juniority gradient §4.6 reports for this cell, read
  on the age clock instead of the birth clock (in-cell, year barely
  moves, so age is very nearly minus birth).
  It lands almost exactly on the COHORT = 0 landmark: a = -0.892 against
  the landmark a = -0.876. So the Senate bound says the panel-wide
  birth gradient is AGE, and the whole same-age decade-on total
  (+2.130) is PERIOD. That is not a new assumption; it is what
  §4.6's own sentence -- 'a juniority gradient in this register predates
  the drift' -- implies once it is carried to the solution line.

  What survives for the cohort reading is NOT this linear term. It is
  the NONLINEARITY -- §4.6's 'steepening as drift-formed cohorts arrive'.
  Nonlinear cohort deviations are identified without any age assumption;
  apc_i.py tests them.

  CAVEATS the cell cannot remove: (i) it identifies the age slope AMONG
  pre-1965 cohorts observed at ages 31-100 in ONE chamber; carrying that
  slope to the whole panel assumes the age profile is linear and
  era-stable -- the residual rival §4.6 already flags, here in the
  direction that costs the cohort reading rather than helping it.
  (ii) c = 0 in-cell is an assumption about pre-1965 cohorts, not a test.
  (iii) the quadrature widening treats the cell estimate as independent
  of the two-stamp fit; the cell's 1,107 rows are inside the two-stamp's
  61,312, so the true covariance is non-zero and that widening is
  indicative only. The age-only bound is the exact one.
