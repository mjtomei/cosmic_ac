# S10 — independent review, 2026-08-11

**This review was run with no access to any prior review of this study.** No earlier
critique was read, requested, or inferred. Where METHODOLOGY.md carries parentheticals of
the form "(review F5)" or "adversarial review found …", those were treated as *unverified
author claims that a problem was handled* and were audited directly rather than credited.

Eight reviewers each took one dimension — calibration/prevalence, genre, Opus screen,
series/cohort, ladder/permeation, quality/DQI, bypass, cross-cutting — reproduced numbers
from the committed artifacts, and read the estimator code. Every finding was then handed
to a separate agent whose only job was to refute it. **89 findings survived refutation:
13 major, 76 minor, 0 critical.** Findings that survived only in part are marked
**PARTIAL**, with the accurate version stated. One major finding was never adversarially
checked because its refuting agent errored; it is quarantined in §2.3 and must be read as
a claim to check, not an established defect.

---

## 1. Verdict

S10 is sound: every headline number in the write-up reproduces from the shipped
artifacts, the calibration arm (0 false positives in 1,260 same-chamber pre-AI controls,
measured chamber by chamber) is a real and well-designed piece of work, and no arm
collapses — nothing here is critical. What does not survive is a set of magnitudes,
denominators and labels: the bypass arm's per-target denominator drops six fully-searched
failed targets and its per-variant interval ignores text clustering (design effect 3.55),
the two lexical-preference estimators share a control-construction bias that roughly
doubles the reported +0.88 and dissolves the "largest at the preference stage" ordering,
and the 12.4% prevalence headline is stated without three conditions that change what it
means (120–360-word band, Mixed pooled with AI, CA-FED genre-stratified). All of it is
repairable by re-estimation and editing rather than new collection — but until it is
fixed, several sentences in §4.3, §4.7, §4.9 and §8 assert more than the artifacts
support, and the study's own standing conventions (one script per claim, cluster your
inference, state the estimand) are violated in places the study elsewhere polices.

#### M1. §4.3's conservativeness argument asserts a fact about its own sample that the sample refutes

**Where.** `S10-WRITEUP-DRAFT.md` §4.3, lines 247–254. **Arm:** genre.

**Claim.** "The sampling runs against the finding, which makes it conservative. … The OQ
cell is therefore the long tail of Question Period, which skews toward *prepared
ministerial answers* rather than rapid exchange — the sub-population most likely to be
machine-drafted. We sampled the most preparable end of the unscripted genre and it still
came in lowest."

**Defect.** The stated mechanism is false in both its compositional and its outcome
halves. 28 of the 60 OQ segments carry a ministerial or parliamentary-secretary title and
they flag **0/28** — the sub-population nominated as "most likely to be machine-drafted"
produced zero flags; all five flags come from non-ministers. The composition claim fails
too: over the full 2025–26 Oral Questions pool (n = 7,459 member-authored English
segments), ministerial share is 46.9% *before* the length filter and 35.4% *after* it,
and the median ministerial utterance is 87 words against 95 for non-ministers — so the
120-word floor selects **away** from ministerial answers, not toward them. What the floor
actually enriches is prepared post-QP business (1.77% of the raw pool, 17.3% of the
in-band pool). The conclusion — that the OQ cell is biased upward and the gradient is
therefore conservative — happens to be true, but by a route the write-up does not
identify and contradicts.

**Evidence.** Tabulating `speaker` against verdict for the 60 OQ/prev rows in
`pangram_p4_verdicts.csv`: 28 segments containing "Minister" or "Secretary", 0 flagged;
32 others, 5 flagged. Retention by rubric item: 82.2% for Business of the House, 64.3%
for Privilege, 36.4% for Points of Order, against 2.8–8.6% for real QP topics.

#### M2. Stage 4's length adjustment is an out-of-support extrapolation, and the sentence built on it does not hold

**Where.** `S10-WRITEUP-DRAFT.md` §4.9 lines 616–619; `quality_expansion/analyze_stage3.py`
lines 160–168. **Arm:** quality/DQI.

**Claim.** "Stage 4 also settles a residual worry from stage 3: justification rises +0.16
raw (t +1.7) and goes negative once word count enters, so the apparent gain was length."

**Defect.** The quoted adjusted coefficient is the regression intercept at a word delta of
zero, and in stage 4 a word delta of zero is essentially not in the data. All 25 paired
deltas are ≥ 0 (mean +29.0, sd 13.8, exactly one at 0), so wd = 0 sits 2.1 sd below the
centre of the sample. Variance inflation for the intercept is 1 + (29.0/13.8)² = 5.43,
predicting an SE inflation of 2.33× — which is exactly what is observed (raw SE 0.16/1.7
= 0.094 against adjusted 0.173/0.8 = 0.216, ratio 2.30). The sign flip from +0.16 to
−0.173 is an extrapolation into a region with one observation, not a demonstration.
Backing out the implied slope gives ~+1.15 justification points per 100 words, 2.4× this
study's own cross-sectional estimate (+0.481), identified off within-pair variation of
±14 words. Stage 3 has no such problem (mean wd −1.3, sd 37.9, VIF 1.00), which is why
its adjusted and raw columns agree — yet the write-up quotes the adjusted column
uniformly and the script instructs "Quote that column."

**Evidence.** `key4.json` paired deltas: all ≥ 0, mean +29.0, sd 13.8, one at 0.
`results_stage34.json` SE ratio 2.30 against the predicted 2.33. Same mechanism gives
stage 4 evidence an adjusted SE of ~0.40 against a raw ~0.11.

**Reproduce.** `python3 /tmp/s10_quality_review.py` (sections F and G), or recompute the
paired deltas from `quality_expansion/key4.json` and the SE ratios from
`quality_expansion/results_stage34.json`.

**Fix.** Centre the covariate at its sample mean, so the reported coefficient is the
effect at typical length (which equals the raw paired mean), and report the length slope
separately. Do not describe the intercept as the effect at equal length when equal length
is outside the data. Delete "so the apparent gain was length" and the script's blanket
"Quote that column." Both columns are null, so the table's conclusion is unchanged — only
the explanatory sentence has to go.

#### M3. The pooled +0.88 alignment effect is roughly double its honest value

**Where.** `S10-WRITEUP-DRAFT.md` §4.7 footnote `[^r47]`; `METHODOLOGY.md` §6.1 line 1335;
`rlhf_pref_analyze.py` TEST A. **Arm:** ladder/permeation.

**Claim.** "Pooled alignment effect +0.88 … prints `EXCESS +0.8797, p < 0.001`."

**Defect.** The number reproduces exactly, but the control set is asymmetric in two
compounding ways. (i) 57 of the 212 admitted style words (26.9%) have **zero** base-model
count and carry mean pref +1.4471, while the control candidate list is built by iterating
`base_c`, so a control word with zero base count is structurally impossible — bucket 0 is
empty and those style words are matched against controls with base count 1–2. The style
mean excluding them is +0.0521. (ii) Controls are bucketed on `int(log2(base_c[w]+1))`,
i.e. selected on the ratio's own denominator, which under a Zipfian vocabulary produces
regression to the mean and drags the control median to −0.4525 where a symmetric control
sits at +0.0020. Four independent corrections converge: symmetric pool +0.3820; symmetric
pool bucketed on the combined count +0.4200; fold-A-selection / fold-B-measurement
+0.3588; and — decisively — feeding the **shipped** procedure 30 pseudo-instrument lists
drawn at random and matched only on the style list's combined frequency returns excess
+0.4297 (sd 0.0695, 30/30 positive). The procedure returns ~+0.42 for word lists with no
special property whatever.

**Evidence.** Reproduced twice independently. Dispersion is *not* the driver: adding a
dispersion tercile to the matching moves the shipped excess only to +0.8959 and the
symmetric excess to +0.4261.

**Reproduce.**
```
cd /home/matt/performance_commons/analysis/s10 && python3 rlhf_pref_analyze.py   # +0.8797
cd /home/matt/performance_commons/analysis/s10 && python3 $SCRATCH/testa.py       # corrections + null calibration
```

**Fix.** Draw controls from `set(base_c) | set(inst_c)` and bucket on
`base_c[w]+inst_c[w]`, or select the bucket on a held-out half of the prompt pairs. Report
the corrected excess (~+0.40) with the null-calibration pedestal stated, and quote the
p-value against the calibrated null rather than against a control distribution displaced
by −0.45. **The arm survives** — +0.42 is ~6.5 sd above the estimator's own pedestal, and
the breadth check corroborates it independently — but the reported magnitude does not.

#### M4. The per-target bypass denominator drops six fully-searched targets that the attack failed on

**Where.** `S10-WRITEUP-DRAFT.md` lines 501–513 (headline table), 457, 464–467;
`BYPASS_METHODOLOGY.md` 251–255. **Arm:** bypass.

**Claim.** "24.6% per target … NB v3 contrastive | 38 | 10 = 26.3% … GO all-31 uniform |
27 | 6 = 22.2% … pooled | 65 | 16 = 24.6% [16, 36]"; "About one flagged speech in four can
be walked past the detector"; "10.6×".

**Defect.** The denominators 38 and 27 count only targets that produced at least one
gate-passing variant, not targets that were attacked. NB v3 searched 40 seeds; two ran 4
and 6 full rounds and never produced a variant under the gate. GO all-31 searched 31
seeds — `BYPASS_METHODOLOGY.md` says "SEARCH ALL 31" in as many words — and four ran 4–6
rounds producing nothing submittable. Those six are attack failures: an adversary who
cannot produce a candidate their own proxy will submit has not walked that speech past
the detector. Corrected: NB v3 10/40 = 25.0% [14.2, 40.2], GO all-31 6/31 = 19.4% [9.2,
36.3], pooled **16/71 = 22.5% [14.4, 33.5]**, strict 15/71 = 21.1%. The vendor multiple
falls from 10.6× to **9.8×**. The same defect appears in Appendix C.4's GO Opus-selected
row, which lists 25 seeds when 35 were searched.

**Evidence.** `bypass_v3.json['state']` has 40 seeds, `['kept']` covers 38; the two
missing ran rounds = 6 (best 56.7) and 4 (best 58.3). `gov_bypass_all.json['state']` has
31 seeds, 27 kept; the four missing ran 4/6/5/6 rounds with bests 50.0, 50.0, 51.7, 51.0.
`gov_bypass_v3.json['state']` has 35 seeds, 25 kept.

**Reproduce.**

**Fix.** Use targets *searched* as the denominator: 10/40, 6/31, pooled 16/71 = 22.5% [14,
34]; strict 21.1%; multiple 9.8×. Adjust the "one in four" phrasing. Report the six (and,
for the Opus-selected run, ten) fully-searched zero-yield targets as a result in their own

#### M5. The per-variant Wilson interval ignores text clustering and is about half the width it should be

**Where.** `S10-WRITEUP-DRAFT.md` lines 493 and 511; `bypass_report.py` 66–73, 157–163.
**Arm:** bypass.

**Claim.** "39 = 8.5% [6.3, 11.4]" and "37 = 8.0% [5.9, 10.9]", Wilson intervals on 461
variants.

**Defect.** Wilson assumes 461 independent Bernoulli trials. The variants are up to 18
rewrites of the same 92 texts, and success is strongly text-clustered — which is precisely
what the methodology's own band analysis asserts (success as a property of the original).
Cluster-robust variance on `seg_id` gives SE 2.441pp against the naive 1.296pp, a design
effect of **3.55**: effective n ≈ 130, not 461. The honest interval is roughly [3.7, 13.2]
(normal, cluster-robust) or [4.0, 13.4] (cluster bootstrap over texts). This matters
because the comparison table places 8.5% "above every vendor adversarial figure" — 2.86%
sits outside [6.3, 11.4] but is much nearer the honest lower bound.

**Evidence.** 92 text clusters, SE 2.441pp, deff 3.55, normal CI [3.68, 13.24]; clustering
on (run, seed) gives 130 clusters, SE 2.408pp, deff 3.45, CI [3.74, 13.18]; non-parametric
cluster bootstrap over texts (20,000 draws, seed 0) gives [4.02, 13.39]. Reproduced
independently. The `wilson()` and `fisher()` implementations themselves are correct — the
defect is the independence assumption, not the estimator code.

**Reproduce.**
```
cd /home/matt/performance_commons/analysis/s10 && python3 -c "
import sys,collections,math; sys.path.insert(0,'.'); import bypass_report as B
rows=B.load(); cl=collections.defaultdict(list)
[cl[r['seg_id']].append(r['verdict']=='Human') for r in rows]
N=sum(len(v) for v in cl.values()); p=sum(sum(v) for v in cl.values())/N; m=len(cl)
var=m/(m-1)*sum((sum(v)-p*len(v))**2 for v in cl.values())/N**2
print(m, math.sqrt(var), math.sqrt(p*(1-p)/N), p-1.96*math.sqrt(var), p+1.96*math.sqrt(var))"
```

**Fix.** Replace the per-variant Wilson interval with a cluster-robust or cluster-bootstrap
interval on text, and say which. Two things bound the damage and should be said too: no
sentence in the arm rests on the interval (the vendor comparisons are point comparisons),
and the per-target 24.6% [16, 36] already has one observation per text, so it is
clustering-clean by construction.

#### M7. The 12.4% headline is a length-band-conditional rate and is never labelled as one — and the study's own §5.0a correction was not carried into the 19-chamber arm

#### M9. §4.7's "largest at the preference stage" is an artifact of a per-transition bias pedestal

**Where.** `S10-WRITEUP-DRAFT.md` §4.7 lines 353–372; `olmo_ladder.py` ARM 1. **Arm:**
ladder/permeation.

**Claim.** "SFT +0.76 / DPO +0.86 / RLVR +0.37 … The shift is largest at the preference
stage, which is what makes §4.5 more than a coincidence of direction: the thing RLHF
selects for is something humans were already drifting toward."

**Defect.** Same estimator defect as M3, and here it differs *by transition*. For each
transition a→b the control candidate pool is `[w for w in C[a] …]` while the style set is
`[w for w in style if C[a][w]+C[b][w] > 0]`, which admits words with `C[a][w]==0` (35/159
at SFT, 69/196 at DPO, 36/214 at RLVR); no control word can occupy that cell, and with
+0.5 smoothing those words score maximally positive. Controls are additionally bucketed on
`C[a]`, the ratio's own denominator, dragging the control median to about −0.40. Null
calibration on pseudo-instrument lists returns pedestals of +0.4548 (SFT), +0.5615 (DPO),
+0.3115 (RLVR) — largest at DPO because DPO's generations are longest. Bias-corrected:
+0.3041 / +0.3010 / +0.0546. A paired bootstrap over the 800 shared generations gives
DPO − SFT = −0.077, 95% CI [−0.305, +0.154], P(DPO ≤ SFT) = 0.757.

**PARTIAL — accurate version.** "Correcting it reverses the ordering" is estimator-
dependent, not established. The reviewer's correction buckets on the *combined* (a+b)
count, which is post-treatment and over-controls. A cross-fitted split-sample correction
(select and bucket on fold-A pre-stage counts, measure on held-out fold B, 16 cross-fits)
puts DPO *above* SFT: +0.2067 vs +0.2407, positive in 11/16 cross-fits. **What every route
agrees on** — reviewer's, refuter's, and an exact stratified estimator with no Monte-Carlo
control draws (+0.3173 / +0.2692 / +0.0602) — is that **SFT and DPO are statistically
indistinguishable and RLVR is near zero**. The section's headline ("the register is a

- Stage-2 conclusions are robust to the Mixed-pooling choice (+0.263 / +0.283 / +0.231
  dropping Mixed).
- The sentinel composite coding is null in all four cells, so Q1's coding choice does not
  overturn the headline.
- Speaker-clustered bootstraps of the Opus AUC CIs widen them only slightly
  ([0.925, 0.973] → [0.917, 0.975]).

## 4. Coverage gaps — what no reviewer could check, and why

**The detector itself.** Nobody could re-score anything against Pangram. Raw text for most
sampled segments is not committed, and Pangram is a closed vendor instrument. Every arm's
verdicts were taken as given; the pipeline was verified forward from
`pangram_p4_verdicts.csv` and backward to the sampling frame, but not through the detector.
The vendor-reported figures in METHODOLOGY §4.3 (RAID 99.3%, 1.4% self-reported FNR) were
not independently verified.

**Ground truth of any kind.** The 241-segment AUC pool contains **no segment of known AI
authorship** — the 40 synthetic Mistral speeches were removed from the study. So every AUC
in §4.4 is concordance with an oracle whose error rate is imported from published
benchmarks, and Opus/Pangram disagreements cannot be attributed to either side from inside
the design. The same limitation is why **O8** cannot be settled empirically.

**Cost and compute.** No token or cost accounting exists for the effort A/B in the
repository; the reviewers could confirm only that 25 agents ran at `effort=low` and 25 at
`effort=max`. The "fifth of the cost" figure was recoverable only from a workflow journal
outside the repo. The max arm's own run-to-run variance is unmeasured by construction.

**GPU passes.** No reviewer re-ran the generation or forward passes
(`olmo_ladder.py score/generate`, `word_context_delta.py score`, `rlhf_pref_generate.py`).
Everything in §4.7/§4.8 is recomputed from cached artifacts, so a defect in generation or
in which checkpoints were actually loaded would be invisible. The Kobak 407-word style list
was taken on trust as an external fixed instrument; `kobak_excess_words.csv` was not audited
against the published list.

**An artifact inconsistency nobody could resolve.** `word_context/lexicons.json` lists 1,044
placebo words while `word_context.log` from the Qwen3 scoring run reports 1,034 — so the
Qwen3 and Mistral occurrence files were produced under slightly different placebo lexicons.
Instrument positions are unaffected (reconstruction matched 15,000/15,000 for both
families), but the placebo DiD in **L6** mixes two placebo definitions. The scripts are not
under version control, so the two versions cannot be diffed.

**The quality arm's raw grades.** Three of the five gradings cannot be recomputed
(**Q12**). Consequences: Appendix C.1's table is unverifiable; stage-3/4 power is bounded
from a rounded cache rather than measured; and the within-pair applicability discordance —
exactly the quantity pairwise deletion throws away — is not computable at all.

**Joins that are blocked.** `results_stage1.json` and `results_stage2.json` omit the segment
id and are not in key order (positional match 176/840, at chance), so the stage-2 length
control (**Q2**) and speaker clustering (**Q8**) cannot be run at all.

**The bypass rewrites.** Variant texts are excluded from the repo under the corpus licence,
so no reviewer could judge whether the 39 reversals preserve argument and factual content,
or whether the plausibility gate was doing real work. The entire quality-preservation side
of the attack is taken on trust. Generated-attempt counts are reconstructed as
3 × Σ`rounds` rather than logged, so the ~1,830 denominator in **M12** is a design count.
`fraction_ai` is recorded for none of the GO Opus-selected seeds and only 11 of 27 GO
all-31 seeds, so the "seeds sit at 1.0" check is solid for NB and partial for GO.

**External sources.** The cross-cutting reviewer's web-search budget was exhausted before
the session began; only direct fetches at known URLs were possible. The Pangram 4 report and
Rice's repository and blog were retrieved and checked. Not verified: the Pimlico Journal
article, the Anthropic watermarking documentation, the Sejm/EU survey, and any of the 22
policy-scan primary sources — §6 and §8.2 are checked only for internal consistency.

**Post-hoc classifications.** The OQ reclassification behind **G1/M1** is post hoc on this
sample (verdicts were seen before the strata were chosen), though the assignment rule uses
only outcome-blind metadata. The procedural/tribute classifier is a regex over `section`;
all 13 OQ/prev hits were hand-checked, the 47 genuine-QP segments were not audited
individually.

**Bio join.** `member_bios.json`'s birth years, occupations and Wikidata matches were taken
at face value; 668 of 1,396 rows are surname-only matches, a live person-blending hazard for
the cohort arm that nobody audited. Birth-year coverage is 79% and plausibly correlated with
prominence, hence with role and tenure — not testable without an external roster.

**Reproducibility friction.** Every documented invocation says `python`; there is no
`python` on this machine and `python3` is required throughout. Each province-panel script
re-reads ~3.6 GB of JSONL from scratch (60–75 s each, five scripts), and `long_trend.py` on
the 3.1 GB deep file takes ~68 s, with no cached intermediate.

## 5. If I were the author, the three things to do first

**1. Fix the bypass arm's two denominators and its interval.** This arm carries four major
findings, three of them confirmed, and they are the only place in the study where reported
headline numbers are simply wrong rather than under-labelled. Change the per-target
denominator to targets *searched* — 16/71 = 22.5% [14.4, 33.5], strict 21.1%, multiple 9.8×
— and report the six fully-searched zero-yield targets as a result in their own right; they
are your only direct evidence of texts the search could not move. Replace the per-variant
Wilson interval with a cluster-bootstrap on text ([4.0, 13.4]); the design effect is 3.55
and the current interval is about half the width it should be. Fix the opening sentence:
92 distinct originals, 106 attacked, not 130. Relabel 8.5% as a post-gate rate and delete
"like-for-like on single attempts the multiple is 3.7×" — and note when you do that the
same-subset version is 4.8×, so honesty costs you nothing here. Add `PLAUS_MIN = 70` to the
parameters table. None of this touches "about one flagged speech in four" or "about
tenfold"; all of it removes the places where a hostile reader gets to say the arithmetic was
convenient.

**2. Rebuild the control pool in the two lexical-preference estimators, and re-run both
arms.** One estimator defect — style words with zero base-model count admitted against a
control pool that cannot contain them, plus bucketing on the ratio's own denominator —
produces a ~+0.42 pedestal in `rlhf_pref_analyze.py` and per-transition pedestals of
+0.45/+0.56/+0.31 in `olmo_ladder.py`. Four independent corrections agree that +0.88 is
really ~+0.40, and the correction dissolves "the shift is largest at the preference stage"
into "SFT and DPO are indistinguishable, RLVR is nothing". Take the second result: your own
preregistered RLVR placebo, shipped at +0.37 with p = 0.000, corrects to +0.05 with a CI
straddling zero, which means the placebo now behaves as designed. That is a better sentence
than the one you have. While you are in there, add the base→instruct row to `TRANSITIONS`
so the footnote's number is printed by the footnote's invocation, add a `--pooled` mode to
`word_context_delta.py` so the permeation interval exists in code, raise its bootstrap to
B ≥ 2,000 with a recorded seed, and lead with the permutation p (0.023) instead of an
endpoint that is inside its own Monte-Carlo error. Then write METHODOLOGY §6.2 for the
ladder — it is the only arm in the study with no methodological write-up and no
"what could have fooled us" section, and this is exactly the bias that section would have
caught.

**3. Say what the 12.4% is a rate of.** Three qualifications belong next to the number, and
none of them requires new data. It is the flag rate in the **120–360-word band**, and your
own §5.0a puts the segment-weighted correction at 1.25–1.7× downward — a direction §5
currently does not admit at all, since "Prevalence is a floor" is the only bias named.
**38% of it is Mixed** (AI-only is 7.7%, and the chamber ordering is not invariant), a split
that appears nowhere in the write-up despite METHODOLOGY insisting on exactly this
distinction — *verify this one yourself, it is the single unrefuted finding in the review*.
And **CA-FED is not a uniform draw**: it is the genre arm at 180 segments with three of at
least eight rubrics, entering the pool at 8.1% weight while §2 says every chamber was
sampled uniformly. All three run conservative, which is why this is a labelling job rather
than a re-estimation, but the headline is the most-quoted sentence in the study and it
currently means something narrower than it says. While you are editing §5, add the
equal-allocation clause (12.4% is the average chamber's rate) and print the omnibus
heterogeneity test (χ² = 88.1 on 17 df, p = 1.4e-11) so the sevenfold-spread claim is
multiplicity-proof on its face.
