READING
==============================================================================
  QUOTE THE ALIGNED FIT. Only there is the age/period/cohort dependency
  exact (unconstrained min-eig ~1e-16), so only there is the constraint,
  rather than the bin edges, doing the identifying -- and only there do
  the two constraint choices provably agree. PRIMARY is reported because
  it is the specified grouping and because it agrees; it is not the
  evidence.

  The global test asks whether the cohort profile is a straight line in
  birth year. Rejecting it is the only cohort claim in §4.6 that no age
  assumption can take away: the LINEAR cohort slope is unidentified (see
  apc_solution_line.py), the curvature is not. It rejects hard.

  The invariant contrasts are the ones to quote. The post-1985 elevation
  and the late-minus-early slope difference are both positive and both
  significant: 'steepening as drift-formed cohorts arrive' stated in a
  form that survives any choice of age slope. This is the part of §4.6
  the solution-line result does NOT take away.

  HONEST COMPLICATION: the deviation profile is U-SHAPED, not a hockey
  stick. Cohorts born in the 1920s and 1930s deviate UPWARD too, by more
  than the post-1985 cohorts do. A pure 'drift-formed cohorts are
  different' story predicts a flat left arm, and this is not flat. Those
  early cohorts are also the panel's sparsest corner -- they appear only
  at old ages in early periods in a few chambers -- so the left arm is
  the weakest part of the estimate, but it is not noise-level: the 1930
  and 1935 deviations carry t > 3.8 on member-clustered errors. Quoted
  as 'the cohort profile is nonlinear, and bends up at both ends', the
  result is solid; quoted as 'it bends up only where the drift-formed
  cohorts are', it is not.
