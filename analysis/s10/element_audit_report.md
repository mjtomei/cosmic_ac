# S10 — blind re-derivation of the U/L/D instrument vs. the hand-built version

Three coders saw each O*NET element independently and assigned it to a cell (U =
upward accountability, L = lateral service, D = downward command) or to nothing.
Elements reaching 2-of-3 are treated as the blind pass's result. The hand-built
instrument holds 19 elements (6 U, 5 L + the subtracted Realistic half, 7 D).

The blind pass placed **48 elements**: 10 in U, 17 in L, 21 in D. It re-derived
**18 of the 19** hand-built elements, and nominated **30** the hand-built version
does not contain.

## 2. MISSED

Elements the blind pass placed that the hand-built instrument does not contain.
Grouped by how seriously they should be taken, not by cell.

### 2a. Worth adding

Same measurement family as the existing instrument (Work Activities / Work Context),
explicitly directional in the item text, 3/3, and not redundant with an element
already in the cell.

| ID | Name | What it measures | Cell | Votes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4.C.1.c.2 | Work Outcomes and Results of Other Workers | Work Context: how responsible the worker is for other workers' outcomes | D | 3/3 |
| 4.C.1.b.1.f | Deal With External Customers or the Public in General | Work Context: importance of dealing with external customers or the public | L | 3/3 |
| 4.A.4.b.6 | Providing Consultation and Advice to Others | Activity: giving expert advice **to management** or other groups | U | 3/3 |

4.C.1.c.2 is the single cleanest downward indicator in O\*NET — it asks directly
about responsibility for other people's work — and the hand-built D contains no
Work Context item at all. 4.C.1.b.1.f is its lateral twin and states the direction
in the item stem, which almost nothing else does. 4.A.4.b.6 is the only activity
element besides 4.A.4.a.2 that names a superior as the counterpart; U is the thin
cell, and it is about to get thinner (§3).

Twenty-one of the thirty misses are Knowledge, Skill, Interest, or Work Style
ratings — capacities and dispositions, not what the job requires the incumbent to
do. The coders flagged nearly all of them as weak evidence themselves. They are
also near-duplicates of activity elements already in the instrument (2.C.1.f
shadows Staffing Organizational Units; 2.C.6 shadows Training and Teaching;
2.B.5.d shadows Guiding/Directing Subordinates; 2.C.1.a shadows the
Management/Administration interest already in D). Adding them would multiply
collinear measures of the same underlying thing and silently reweight the
composite toward D — which is already the cell the blind pass over-nominates
(21 elements vs. 10 for U).

Two specific warnings. First, the three non-activity U nominations (Conventional
interest, Office Work interest, Administrative knowledge) all measure clerical and
paperwork content. That is exactly the confound the challenge to Letters and Memos
names (§4) — adding them would reimport through the back door the problem that
dropping Letters and Memos is meant to remove. Second, 4.C.1.d.2 (angry people)
indexes police and retail work, not the direction of an interaction; it would put
occupational unpleasantness into a lateral-service score.

## 3. NOT RE-DERIVED

| ID | Name | Cell | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4.C.1.a.2.j | Written Letters and Memos | U | Nominated by **1 of 3** coders — below threshold. Not unmentioned: it was seen and rejected, and separately drew the largest set of seconded objections in the audit (7). |

That is the only hand-built element the blind pass failed to reproduce. Every other
element was placed, in the right cell, by at least two of three coders.

Two other below-threshold nominations, for context, were elements *not* in the
hand-built instrument: 4.C.1.b.1.e (Work With or Contribute to a Work Group or
Team, L) and 4.C.3.a.2.b (Frequency of Decision Making, U), each 1 of 3.

## 4. CHALLENGED

| ID | Name | Seconds | Objection | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4.C.1.a.2.j | Written Letters and Memos | 7 | Two distinct problems. (i) No direction: the item asks only how *often* the job requires written correspondence; memos go to clients, the public, and subordinates as readily as to superiors. (ii) Circularity: it measures how writing-heavy a job is, and the outcome is a written bureaucratic register — a hit would say wordy jobs breed wordy speech, not that upward accountability does. This is the confound the hypothesis explicitly disclaims. | **Drop.** It failed the blind re-derivation *and* drew the heaviest objection; both point the same way. |
| 4.A.4.b.3 | Training and Teaching Others | 3 | The item does not specify who is taught. For teachers and nurses — occupations that matter in these chambers — the people instructed are students and patients served laterally, not subordinates commanded, so keeping it in D codes teachers as commanders. | **Keep in D, but test it.** It got 3/3 in D on blind coding, so the objection is a hypothesis, not a finding. Check empirically whether it loads with the supervisory D items or with L, and report the composite with it dropped as a robustness check. |
| 4.A.4.a.2 | Communicating with Supervisors, Peers, or Subordinates | 2 | One rating pools all three directions, so a pure supervisor scores high on it; it cannot separate upward from downward communication. | **Keep, downweight.** Both objectors concede nothing cleaner exists in O\*NET, and it is the only activity element that names supervisors at all. Treat it as diluted evidence and say so in the write-up. |

## 5. Verdict

The hand-built instrument is substantially reproduced. Eighteen of nineteen
elements came back in the same cell, and the two least obvious choices — the
reverse-scored autonomy pair and the Social-minus-Realistic composite — were
re-derived with their rationale intact. The single element that failed to reproduce
is also the single most-objected-to element, which is convergent rather than
coincidental. The blind pass is not evidence that the instrument should grow: three
coders at 2-of-3 is weak evidence, most of what they added is a different kind of
measurement, and thirty additions to nineteen elements would change what the
instrument measures rather than measure it better.

Three changes worth making:

1. **Drop 4.C.1.a.2.j (Written Letters and Memos) from U.** Direction-free, and
   confounded with the outcome. This is the highest-value change in the audit.
2. **Add the two directional Work Context items:** 4.C.1.c.2 (Work Outcomes and
   Results of Other Workers) to D and 4.C.1.b.1.f (Deal With External Customers or
   the Public) to L. Both state the direction in the item stem, which almost nothing
   else in O\*NET does, and both cells currently rely entirely on self-reported
   activity frequencies.
3. **Add 4.A.4.b.6 (Providing Consultation and Advice to Others) to U**, which keeps
   U at six elements after the drop and gives it a second item that names a superior
   as the recipient.

Everything else is noise or construct-widening. The Knowledge, Skill, Interest, and
Work Style nominations should stay out: they are dispositions and capacities rather
than job demands, they duplicate activity items already present, and they would tilt
the composite toward D. The one thing the blind pass surfaces that no single edit
fixes is that U is thin in O\*NET generally — the coders found 21 downward elements
and 10 upward ones, and half the upward ones are reverse-scored discretion. That is a
limitation to state in the write-up, not something to paper over by adding weak items.
