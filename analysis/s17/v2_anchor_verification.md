# S17 v2 — anchor verification for the four-leg argument (2026-08-06, 3 web agents)

Verification for `plans/S17-proposed-text.md` v2. Everything below was checked
against primary or publisher-of-record sources except where flagged. Sibling
ledgers: `externality_anchor_verification.md`, `negative_control.md`.

## Leg 1 — the ledger (social vs private returns; negative under full accounting)

- **Jones & Summers** — CITATION CORRECTED: NBER WP **27863** (2020; ch.1 of
  *Innovation and Public Policy*, U Chicago 2022), not 25712. Verbatim: "a
  conservative estimate of the average social gains is about $5 in benefit
  per $1 invested," plausible accountings "$10 or even $20 per $1." Baseline
  benefit-cost ratio 13.3 (social IRR 67%); conservative corrections 4.0–5.3.
  Caveat: growth-accounting calibration, average returns; marginal extension
  (3.7–11.4) model-dependent.
- **Bloom, Schankerman & Van Reenen**, Econometrica 81(4):1347–1393, 2013,
  doi:10.3982/ECTA9466. Gross marginal social return **55.0%** vs private
  **20.7%** (preferred spec, Table IX); "socially optimal level of R&D is
  about three times as large as the observed level." Gap "probably a lower
  bound."
- **Lucking, Bloom & Van Reenen**, Fiscal Studies 40(4):561–590, 2019,
  doi:10.1111/1475-5890.12195. Through 2015: social **57.7%** vs private
  **13.6%** — ratio ~4:1, widened from 3:1 mostly via a lower private return.
- **Bessen & Meurer, *Patent Failure*** (Princeton 2008). For US public firms
  OUTSIDE chem/pharma, by the late 1990s: litigation costs ~**$12B/yr** vs
  aggregate patent profits ~**$3B/yr** (1992 dollars) — costs ~4× rents; the
  crossover is mid-1990s (B&M's own FTC-presentation chart). CAVEATS
  (mandatory): "litigation cost" is event-study market-value loss of alleged
  infringers (includes expected damages, partly transfers); rents from
  Tobin's-q-style regressions; public firms only; chem/pharma exception is
  integral; contested (Schwartz & Kesan). Cite as "contested" if load-bearing.
- **Measured deadweight case**: Chaudhuri, Goldberg & Jia, AER 96(5):
  1477–1514, 2006, doi:10.1257/aer.96.5.1477 (quinolones, India). Under price
  regulation: total welfare loss ~$305M/yr, consumer loss ~$255M, foreign
  patent-holder gain ~$19.6M — loss exceeds the holder's gain by an order of
  magnitude. ALWAYS name the scenario (unregulated variant: ~$713M vs ~$57M).
  Boldrin & Levine (CUP 2008) is argument-only, no canonical number — cite
  for the argument, never a figure.
- **Arrow 1962** read in full this repo (`reading/notes/arrow-1962.md`):
  underinvestment (p.619) AND underutilization (p.617) verbatim — the selling
  regime loses on both margins, stated at the origin.

## Leg 2 — power abusing the protective systems

- **Bessen & Meurer, Cornell L Rev 99:387 (2014)**: NPE direct costs **$29B
  in 2011** (up from ~$7B in 2005) — outside legal services + license/
  settlement fees (fees only ~¼ of total), excludes indirect costs (their
  event-study total: ~$80B/yr). CAVEATS (mandatory): RPX-run survey, 82
  self-selected respondents, extrapolated; Coalition for Patent Fairness
  helped fund; Schwartz & Kesan (same issue, 99:425) call it "substantially
  overstated" and note settlements are partly transfers. Cite as contested.
- **Eldred / economists' brief** — VERIFIED, and the prize: **Kenneth Arrow
  signed** (with Akerlof, Buchanan, Coase, Friedman — five Nobels among 17).
  Brief arithmetic verbatim: the CTEA's term extension is "a 0.33% increase
  in present-value payments to the author" for new works (0.47% works for
  hire); for existing works "no significant contribution to an author's
  economic incentive"; a deadweight loss today is "224 times as large in
  present value as a deadweight loss eighty years from now." CTEA 1998
  (Pub. L. 105-298) retroactivity confirmed. CAVEAT: the economists lost —
  Eldred v. Ashcroft, 537 U.S. 186 (2003), 7–2, only Breyer's dissent
  adopted the analysis. (Counter-critique exists: Liebowitz & Margolis,
  Harv JOLT 18:435, 2005.) FRAMING NOTE: the worked example is years
  81–100, not 56–95.
- **Machlup 1958**, p.80 of Senate Study No. 15, exact: "If we did not have a
  patent system, it would be irresponsible, on the basis of our present
  knowledge of its economic consequences, to recommend instituting one. But
  since we have had a patent system for a long time, it would be
  irresponsible, on the basis of our present knowledge, to recommend
  abolishing it." Adjacent: the "muddle through" sentence precedes it; a
  scope caveat (applies to the US, not small/nonindustrial countries)
  follows.
- **Cohen-Nelson-Walsh 2000** — FRAMING CORRECTED: do NOT write "firms patent
  for blocking more than protection." Prevent-copying is first (95.8%
  product), blocking second (81.8%). The defensible claims: strategic motives
  (blocking/negotiation/suit-prevention) cited by large majorities, heavy
  patenters disproportionately strategic (suits 74%, negotiations 58%
  application-weighted); patents rank last/near-last among appropriability
  mechanisms in most industries; complex-product industries use patents as
  negotiation chips (54.9% vs 40.6% discrete) — executive quote: "Mostly,
  your patents are used in horse trading."
- **Heller & Eisenberg**, Science 280(5364):698–701 (1998),
  doi:10.1126/science.280.5364.698; Heller, Harv L Rev 111:621 (1998) —
  anticommons: fragmented upstream rights → underuse by mutual blocking.
- **Publishing capture**: RELX Annual Report 2024 (SEC 6-K): Scientific,
  Technical & Medical segment £1,172M adjusted operating profit on £3,051M
  revenue = **38.4% margin** (computed per RELX's own APM; RELX doesn't
  print the percentage; segment broader than journals — flag both).
  Larivière, Haustein & Mongeon, PLoS ONE 10(6):e0127502 (2015): top five
  publishers >50% of Web of Science papers (2013); verbatim: "publishers
  neither pay the provider of the primary good—authors of scholarly
  papers—nor for the quality control—peer review." Their own Elsevier margin
  series (38.9% avg 2006–13) converges with the 2024 primary.

## Legs 3–4 — the credit economy and the open estimator

- **Merton**, ASR 22(6):635–659 (1957), read against the scan. Verbatim
  p.640: "property rights in science become whittled down to just this one:
  the recognition by others of the scientist's distinctive part in having
  brought the result into being." Eponymy as the head of the reward forms
  (p.642).
- **Dasgupta & David**, Research Policy 23(5):487–521 (1994),
  doi:10.1016/0048-7333(94)01002-1. Two reward systems over the same
  non-rival knowledge: priority-of-disclosure vs secrecy-for-rent. FLAG: the
  two-regimes sentence verified via summaries only; pull full text before
  quoting verbatim.
- **Shapley** — PHRASING CORRECTED: write "computing the Shapley value is
  #P-complete even for games as simple as weighted majority games (Deng &
  Papadimitriou, Math OR 19(2):257–266, 1994)"; NOT "#P-hard in general"
  (their own abstract shows a tractable graph-game class). Ghorbani & Zou,
  "Data Shapley," ICML 2019 (PMLR 97:2242–2251) — Monte Carlo approximation
  in practice. Survey: Rozemberczki et al., IJCAI 2022, doi:10.24963/
  ijcai.2022/778.
- **Performing-rights precedent** — the gift: *BMI v. CBS*, 441 U.S. 1, 4–5
  (1979), the Supreme Court on ASCAP's 1914 founding: performances "so
  fleeting, that as a practical matter it was impossible for the many
  individual copyright owners to negotiate with and license the users and to
  detect unauthorized uses" — the institution founded on measurement cost.
  Sampling: Massarsky (CNI 1993): ASCAP's radio sample ~60,000 hours = 0.1%
  of broadcast hours, "99.9% of all performances go undetected." ASCAP's own
  standing rule: census "when the cost of collecting and processing accurate
  data is a low enough percentage of the licensing revenue," sample
  otherwise. Streaming: census-based for matched works, NUANCE: unmatched
  low-play works can still be sampled — write "shifted from sampling toward
  census as measurement cost collapsed." User-centric vs pro-rata: CNM/
  Deloitte Jan 2021 (Spotify+Deezer France data): no massive aggregate
  shift; top-10 artists −12.5 to −17.2%, classical ~+24%, rap/hip-hop ~−20%
  — the valuation dispute arriving on schedule once measurement is solved.
- **Intelligence-cost trend** — PHRASING CORRECTED: Epoch AI data insight,
  "LLM inference prices have fallen rapidly but unequally across tasks"
  (2025-03-12): price to reach fixed performance milestones fell **9×–900×
  per year** depending on task (e.g., GPT-4-level GPQA Diamond: 40×/yr);
  Epoch's caveat: fastest drops are recent, persistence unclear. Do NOT use
  the "~1000× in 2 years" variant (that's a16z's LLMflation, unverified
  here).
- **Arrow 1962 for leg 4** (read in full): the separation sentence (p.617,
  reward "completely separated from any charge to the users") and the
  attribution system declared impossible (pp.617–618, "unimaginably complex
  and subtle," "elaborate distinctions of partial property rights of all
  degrees") — the leg-4 hinge; see `reading/notes/arrow-1962.md`.
