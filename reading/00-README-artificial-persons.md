# Artificial Persons — reading list

The lineage behind **the tulpa thesis** (V.3c's response to Teles + V.3's
office-is-a-socket beat, Matthew, 2026-08-06, verbatim in
`outline-cosmic-ac.md`): the United States president "is not exactly a
sovereign individual but is expected to be a kind of **tulpa for the leader of
the United States**," and rotation between humans is "a way to avoid too much
of the individual leaking into the instantiation." Term limits as
regularisation.

**The unifying claim (stated in both directories; Durkheim is the hinge):**
god, king, corporation, and state are the same object — a collective
intelligence personified, with humans as the instantiation medium. The machine
version is the first instantiation that can show its work.

Split 2026-08-07 from a joint directory at Matthew's direction, after the
citation-graph pass showed the two theses pull in disjoint literatures: this
directory holds the doctrine-and-proof half (law, political theory, analytic
philosophy, social choice, cybernetics); sibling **`the-shared-lie/`** holds
thesis 2 (the watching god, its measurement program, and the panopticon with
its inversion). Other siblings: `governance-and-state-capacity/`,
`democratization-of-counsel/`, `the-trustworthy-firm/`. `notes/` is shared.

## The three clusters

**1. The doctrine: persons that are not people.** Kantorowicz, *The King's Two
Bodies* (Princeton, 1957 — purchase list; Shortall 2023 on disk as the
reassessment) — body natural vs body politic, the office-persona that "never
dies"; Hobbes, *Leviathan* ch. 16 — "it is the Unity of the Representer, not
the Unity of the Represented, that maketh the Person One" — and **ch. 25, "Of
Counsell," a specification for machine counsel in 1651** (counsellors as the
artificial person's memory; private ends as the named defect; "Rash, And
Unevident Inferences … Are Repugnant To The Office Of A Counsellour" —
show-your-work as a duty of office); *Dartmouth v. Woodward* (1819) — Story's
instantiation-medium sentence at 667, Marshall making rotation the *point* of
the device at 636. Incoming from the citation graph: Maitland 1903 (the
seminal root), Runciman 1997 (*Pluralism and the Personality of the State*),
Lefort (democracy disincorporates the king's body — power as an empty place
filled by rotation), Kahn 2009.

**2. Group agency, made formal.** French 1979 (the CID structure launders the
individual out of the collective act — the discretion was never located in a
human); List 2018. Incoming: the **discursive dilemma in OA journal form**
(List & Pettit 2002 "Aggregating Sets of Judgments"; Kornhauser & Sager 1986;
Pettit 2001) — the tulpa result as a theorem; **List 2021 "Group Agency and
Artificial Intelligence"** — the framework's own author building the machine
bridge; the moral-agency contest (Velasquez 1983/2003, Copp 2006, Sepinwall
2016 survey; Björnsson & Hess 2016; Bryson-Diamantis-Grant 2017 as the
objection lane).

**3. Cybernetics and the handover.** Beer's VSM (with Allende correcting Beer
that System Five was not the president but the people — the coup as the failure
mode); Runciman's LRB essay ("Corporations are another form of artificial
thinking machine"); **Davies now on disk in OA essay form** —
`davies-2025-taming-unaccountability-machine-hypertext.pdf` (★★ ⚠, ranked by the
Runciman standard), his fullest open statement of accountability sinks
("when the feedback link is broken between the consequences of decisions and
the body responsible for making them, learning is almost impossible") with the
corporations-as-AI framing verbatim in the appended Back of Mind post
("organisations, corporations and governments can be seen as 'artificial
intelligences', in the sense that they're non human decision making systems").
The objection carried in I.2/V.3c can now be quoted in Davies' own words rather
than through Teles's secondhand account; the book stays on the purchase list as
the citable form. Incoming: Espejo 1980 (Cybersyn at
source), Ulrich's critique + Beer's reply — a paper proposing machine counsel
in governance must carry that critique.

## The reading path — the tulpa argument in order
(Entries and numbering from the joint pass; entries 1 and 8 point into
`the-shared-lie/`.)

### Stage I — The claim: god, king, corporation and state are one object

**1. `../the-shared-lie/durkheim-1912-elementary-forms.pdf` ★★★ (file lives in the sibling directory)**
The joint path opens with Durkheim — the god of the clan *is* the clan,
personified (Bk. II ch. VII §II, [Pg 347]: "the sacred beings, though superior
to men, can live only in the human consciousness") — because it states the
unifying claim as a published sociological thesis. Durkheim is the **hinge work**
between this directory and `the-shared-lie/`: read it first for either path.

**2. `hobbes-1651-leviathan.pdf` ★★★**
The same object as engineering. The Introduction's "Artificiall Man" anatomy already assigns counsellors the role of memory; ch. 16 gives the tulpa thesis its formal statement ("it is the Unity of the Representer, not the Unity of the Represented, that maketh the Person One"); ch. 19 names the rotation problem as "Artificiall Eternity." Then read **ch. 25, "Of Counsell"** — the unexpected find of the batch, and effectively a specification for machine counsel: counsellors with "their particular ends" are the defect, and "Rash, And Unevident Inferences ... Are Repugnant To The Office Of A Counsellour" is show-your-work as a duty of office.

**3. `dartmouth-v-woodward-1819.pdf` ★★★**
The doctrine alive and still controlling. Marshall at 636 makes rotation the *point* of the device — "a perpetual succession of many persons are considered as the same, and may act as a single individual" — and Story at 667 supplies the instantiation-medium sentence outright. This is what lets the paper stand on law instead of asserting the identity as metaphor. Keep Marshall's deflationary limit in view: the corporation is a creature of charter, explicitly *not* a participant in civil government.

**4. `shortall-2023-kings-two-bodies-secularization.pdf` ★★★ [OBJECTION]**
Read immediately after 3, while the genealogy is still fresh. Gives a reliable summary of the king's two bodies (body politic outranking body natural; Plowden's "as to this Body the King never dies"), then argues that theological genealogies prove less than they are used to prove: "even if a modern political concept can be shown to derive from theological origins, it does not follow that the concept continues to operate theologically in the present" (p. 806). **This is the best-credentialled published statement of why shared descent is not identity**, and carrying it costs the paper nothing, because the real load is borne by the mechanism (personified office, rotating medium), not by the genealogy. Print it; do not route around it.

### Stage II — The mechanism: how the office keeps the individual out

**5. `french-1979-corporation-moral-person.pdf` ★★★**
The mechanism the tulpa thesis needs, published in 1979 so the paper inherits rather than invents. The CID Structure — chart plus recognition rules — exists to launder the individual out of the collective act: personal reasons are "diluted by the subordination of individual inputs to peer group input," and the corporation still has reasons even when a voter has none ("X did not join the cartel, Gulf Oil Corporation joined the cartel," p. 214). This is the direct answer to Teles: the discretion did not vanish when you looked for the human holding it; **it was never located in a human.** Cite the CID mechanism, not the contested moral-personhood conclusion. Note for Stage III: the CID Structure is a redescription licence, not a record — it explains that the corporation had reasons without ever exhibiting them.

**6. `list-2018-group-agent-what-is-it-like.pdf` ★★★ [OBJECTION]**
The analytic form of "a collective intelligence personified": group awareness is real on functionalist grounds, and — the show-your-work point — third-personally analysable in a way human awareness is not. Then the objection, and it is sharp: no phenomenal consciousness, therefore only *derivative* rights and "especially strict checks and controls" (p. 37). Applied to machine counsel that cuts directly against granting the instantiation standing of its own. It also carries its own contest honestly (Schwitzgebel, Huebner, Briggs) and is conditional on IIT, which List himself flags as controversial. **Caveat for planning: this is not the discursive-dilemma paper — List sets the aggregation machinery aside at p. 8.** See Gaps.

**7. `beer-vsm-provenance.pdf` ★★★**
The same-object claim made as a scientific claim, with the metaphor reading explicitly refused: the rules "apply to an organism such as a human being, or to an organization consisting of human beings such as the State ... not by way of analogy." Beer pre-empts the free-will objection — "What matters is the functioning of an element ... not the identity of the element itself" — and then, at p. 10, states the tulpa thesis about a sitting head of state: Allende correcting him that System Five was not the president but the people, with the coup named as the failure mode. Also the cleanest published account of the polity as a *designed* nervous system, which is what machine counsel proposes to redesign.

**8. `../the-shared-lie/plato-republic-jowett.pdf` (file in sibling)** — ★★ *(skippable)***
Read 416d–417b here, not with the noble lie: guardians may hold no private property, no closed house, no gold, on pain of becoming "housekeepers and husbandmen instead of guardians" — an office designed to suppress its occupant, twenty-three centuries before term limits. The 414b material belongs to Stage III; see Side tracks.

**9. `runciman-corporations-ai-essay.pdf` ★★ *(skippable)* [OBJECTION]**
The bridge into the show-your-work claim, in four quotable sentences: corporations are "another form of artificial thinking machine," and three hundred years of law has produced "little progress in understanding what goes on inside the black box of the corporate mind." The tulpa thesis in journalistic form — "Kalanick is not Uber — he goes, but the business goes on." Skippable because French and List make every substantive claim more rigorously; keep it as the OA verification anchor for the Runciman/*Handover* line the outline already flags. Do not print the Thurlow quotation without independent verification — it is Runciman's paraphrase of an apocryphal line.

## Gaps (numbering preserved from the joint pass; 8–10 live in the sibling)

Excluding what the purchase list already covers (Kantorowicz 1957, List & Pettit 2011, Norenzayan 2013, Runciman 2023, Davies 2024, Brin 1998, Foucault 1975, Zuckerman 2008):

1. **The discursive dilemma is missing outright.** The README's cluster-2 framing expects "the tulpa result as a theorem," and List 2018 explicitly declines to supply it, pointing to List & Pettit 2011 — which is on the purchase list and unavailable. The judgment-aggregation result has OA primary literature predating the book (the List & Pettit and Pettit journal papers of 2001–02); acquire one of those rather than waiting on the monograph. Without it, the claim that a collective's reasoning is structurally not its members' reasoning rests on French's redescription licence alone.

2. **Cybersyn as the socialist-calculation debate in hardware is unsourced.** It is not in the Beer file — Chile appears only as provenance. Beer's own reference list points at Espejo (1980a) for the operations-room and economic-calculation material; that is the acquisition.

3. **The technocratic-control critique of the VSM is named but absent.** Beer's references include his reply to Ulrich's "Critique of pure cybernetic reason: the Chilean experiment with cybernetics." A paper proposing machine counsel in governance must carry that critique rather than cite Beer clean. Get Ulrich's original *and* Beer's reply.

4. **Nothing measures rotation.** Term limits as regularisation is thesis 1's operative claim, and the directory supports it only doctrinally (Dartmouth's perpetual succession, Hobbes's artificial eternity). There is no empirical work here on whether rotation actually prevents the individual leaking into the office — the legislative term-limits literature, or the executive-succession literature, is a real hole.

5. **Teles is answered at second hand.** The objection that automating cognition "pulls out where the actual discretion and judgment are" is the argument Stage II exists to meet, and the source is not in the directory. Acquire the Teles piece so the rebuttal quotes it accurately.

6. **Nothing on machine counsel as practised.** No work here on advice-taking, algorithmic aversion, or automated decision support inside bureaucracies. The whole applied half of both theses currently rests on Hobbes ch. 25 as its only specification.

7. **"Shows its work" has no modern legal counterpart.** Hobbes's duty to propound advice "in such forme of speech, as may make the truth most evidently appear" is the directory's only account of what showing work consists of. The administrative-law reason-giving literature — duties to state grounds, reviewability of unexplained decisions — is the natural companion and is entirely absent. Note also that French's CID Structure is explicitly *not* a record, which sharpens the need.


11. **Housekeeping, not scholarship:** the Dartmouth scan is dirty OCR of a whole U.S. Reports volume and the Bentham has no text layer at all — every quotation from either must be proofed against the page image before it reaches print.

### (c) The revised reading path

Six stages, **34 numbered stops**, objections placed with what they contest
rather than quarantined. The five ★ entries are off the path entirely (listed at
the end). Stages I–II are the claim and the persona; III is the proof; IV
measures it; V is the handover and its critique; VI is the forward edge.

#### Stage I — The claim: god, king, corporation and state are one object

**1. `../the-shared-lie/durkheim-1912-elementary-forms.pdf` ★★★** *(sibling directory)*
Unchanged as the opening: the god of the clan *is* the clan, personified
(Bk. II ch. VII §II). The hinge work between this directory and
`the-shared-lie/`; read it first for either path.

**2. `maitland-1903-moral-legal-personality.pdf` ★★★** — **NEW, promoted to the front.**
Seventeen pages, and it states the arithmetic the whole directory runs on:
"if n men unite themselves in an organised body, jurisprudence, unless it wishes
to pulverise the group, must see n + 1 persons" (p. 316), grounded not in law
but in moral sentiment. Nusquamia — "the largest 'collection' of zeros is only
zero" (p. 318) — is the irreducibility premise Copp and French later formalise.
Reading it here means the paper builds on the root rather than re-deriving it.

**3. `hobbes-1651-leviathan.pdf` ★★★**
The same object as engineering. Introduction (the "Artificiall Man" anatomy,
counsellors as memory); ch. 16 ("the Unity of the Representer … maketh the
Person One"); ch. 19 ("Artificiall Eternity"); then **ch. 25 "Of Counsell"** —
private ends as the named defect, and "Rash, And Unevident Inferences … Are
Repugnant To The Office Of A Counsellour" as show-your-work stated as a duty of
office in 1651.

**4. `kantorowicz-1951-pro-patria-mori-ahr-mgh-scan.pdf` ★★★** — **NEW.**
The genealogy in Kantorowicz's own primary words, and the single hinge the
unifying claim needs: the *corpus mysticum* migrating from church to state, and
Innocent IV's *persona ficta* as "that abstraction of any aggregate of man" (p.
486) — the direct upstream of Maitland, Dartmouth and French. Read its closing
pages (491–92) too: the doctrine run forward to the Tombs of Martyrs, i.e. the
device with a body count. *Support reading, same sitting:*
`kantorowicz-kings-two-bodies-princeton-sample-introduction.pdf` ★★ (four pages;
the "man-made irreality" sentence) and, if the framing question comes up,
`leyser-2016-…` ★★.

**5. `dartmouth-v-woodward-1819.pdf` ★★★**
The doctrine alive and controlling. Marshall at 636 makes rotation the *point*
of the device; Story at 667 supplies the instantiation-medium sentence. Keep
Marshall's deflationary limit in view. *Follow with*
`sepinwall-2012-citizens-united-corporate-citizenship.pdf` ★★ if the
constitutional pressure point matters.

**6. `rogin-1979-kings-two-bodies-presidential-self-sacrifice.pdf` ★★★**
Kantorowicz applied to the American presidency directly — the tulpa thesis's
nearest published relative on its actual subject.

**7. `kahn-2009-political-theology-fiction-kings-two-bodies.pdf` ★★★**
The doctrine as an *authored, maintained* artifact, which is what makes it a
design question rather than a survival. Read immediately after Rogin.

**8. The genealogy objections — read as a block, here, not later.**
- `shortall-2023-kings-two-bodies-secularization.pdf` ★★★ ⚠ — theological
  descent does not prove present theological operation (p. 806). The
  best-credentialled statement of why shared descent is not identity.
- `isseroff-2023-schmitt-political-theology-analogy-religions.pdf` ★★ ⚠ —
  **NEW, and the sharper of the two.** Arendt's functionalization charge:
  "Two ideas that 'do' the same thing are thereby considered identical" is a
  flight from experience, and the man with the gun is "always only playing God,
  just as a high-heeled shoe is only ever playing at being a hammer." This is
  aimed straight at *one object*, and answering it forces the useful precision —
  the claim is about a shared mechanism of instantiation, not a rational analogy
  between concepts.
- `schmitt-1922-political-theology-ch1-3.pdf` ★★ ⚠ — **NEW.** Read for ch. 3's
  secularization thesis (the claim everyone else is arguing inside) and, more
  importantly, ch. 2's objection to the forward edge: "The legal prescription …
  only designates how decisions should be made, not who should decide" (p. 33).
  Reason-giving covers the how and never the who. Carry the provenance cost
  openly.

*Off-path support for this stage:* `bento-kantorowicz-…` ★★ (the 1955
"Mysteries of State" essay at second hand — acts valid *ex opere operato*
regardless of the occupant's worth, plus the maxims/coups reason-giving

#### Stage II — The persona: how the office keeps the individual out

**9. `lefort-permanence-theologico-political.pdf` ★★★** — **NEW, and the best
single acquisition for the thesis's second beat.** Power as an empty place:
"power belongs to no one; … those who exercise power do not possess it; … they

**10. `goffman-1956-presentation-of-self.pdf` ★★★** — **NEW.** The same claim at
the scale of a person walking into a job, and via Durkheim's own term: the front

an actor takes on an established social role, usually he finds that a particular
front has already been established for it" (pp. 16–17). Then pp. 10–12 document
the exact leakage rotation exists to prevent. Four pages carry it; read
selectively.

**11. `runciman-2023-handover-publisher-preview-introduction.pdf` ★★★** — **NEW,

constitutes a powerful artificial creature that cannot be reduced to either
component") and the replicability argument at pp. 7–8 (modern organisations
"can work regardless of the people and places they have to work with") — the
empirical content of the tulpa claim, and the reason term limits are possible at
all. The LRB essay is now a skippable duplicate.

**12. `morgan-1978-great-political-fiction-nyrb.pdf` ★★** — **NEW.** The
operating rule for a fiction: not "false, therefore discard" but nourish it by
making the facts resemble it — the 1628 Commons enforcing the office against the
man, "Give a king a good name and then make him live up to it."
**Read with its objection:** `barnett-2014-we-the-people-…` ★★ ⚠ — the
personified collective cannot bind the humans it is made of; "the sovereign,
when traced to his source, must be found in the man" (Wilson, *Chisholm*).
Barnett is Velasquez played against constitutional law rather than the
corporation, and he supplies a worked case of the fiction abused (Ackerman's
People as an accountability sink).

#### Stage III — The proof: group agency and the aggregation theorems

**13. `french-1979-corporation-moral-person.pdf` ★★★**
The mechanism, published in 1979 so the paper inherits rather than invents: the
CID Structure exists to launder the individual out of the collective act. Cite
the mechanism, not the contested moral-personhood conclusion. Note the limit
that Stage VI turns on: the CID Structure is a redescription **licence, not a
record** — it explains that the corporation had reasons without ever exhibiting
them.

**14. `list-pettit-2002-aggregating-judgments.pdf` ★★★** — **NEW. Gap 1 closed.**
The theorem itself (p. 100), and — more useful — §4's corollary: groups do not
escape the result, they "ensconce the rule of rationality in their collective
lives … only at a cost in the extent to which they are responsive to the views
of their members" (p. 101). The office departing from its occupants, proved.
Note the complication for rotation: the standard escape (the *authority
strategy*) is to let one person's judgment stand for the group's — the
individual leaking into the instantiation by design.

**15. `pettit-2001-groups-with-minds.pdf` ★★★ ⚠** — **NEW.** The inference from
theorem to personhood, and the mechanism derived rather than stipulated: a group
answerable over time builds "a history of judgments that it is on record as
making," and inconsistency makes it "a laughing-stock." The discontinuity at
full strength (p. 20–21). **The objection is in the same file and belongs
printed:** Pettit rejects the possession model outright — persons taking over
psychologies "in the way demons are said to assume possession of souls" is
"clearly crazy," and natural persons keep "an inescapable priority" (p. 28).
It costs the thesis nothing (the tulpa claim is about where judgment is formed,
not who drives the body) and pre-empts the obvious reply.

**16. `kornhauser-sager-1993-one-and-many.pdf` ★★★ ⚠** — **NEW.** The theorem as
an event in an institution of state: *Tidewater*, where "each justification
offered was in fact rejected by a substantial majority of the Court" (p. 25).
The highest court in the country, under the most demanding reason-giving norm in
American law, structurally unable to show its work. **Its objection travels with
it:** neither voting protocol is always superior, the paradox has victims, and
the jurists who found it declined Pettit's clean rule in favour of a
case-by-case "metavote." Load-bearing pages only: 11–13, 13–20, 24–26.
*Priority citation, not a read:* `kornhauser-sager-1986-…` ★★ (p. 115).

**17. `pettit-2018-consciousness-incorporated.pdf` ★★★** — **NEW, and the
mechanism paragraph of the tulpa thesis written by a principal.** The authorized
voice is "not borrowed from elsewhere"; members "make it up as they go along";
it "expresses a mind that they bring into existence for the first time" (p. 15).
Crucially for rotation: identity is fixed by the group's attitudes plus its
updating procedure, not by which humans occupy the nodes.

**18. `list-2018-group-agent-what-is-it-like.pdf` ★★★ ⚠**
Read directly against 17 — the pair bounds what can honestly be claimed about
whether anyone is home in an office. Pettit affirms access-consciousness; List
denies phenomenal consciousness and therefore grants only derivative rights plus
"especially strict checks and controls" (p. 37). Applied to machine counsel that
cuts against granting the instantiation standing of its own.

**19. `list-2021-group-agency-ai.pdf` ★★★** — **NEW, and the identity the thesis
asserts, stated by the framework's own author:** "group agents can be viewed as
special cases of AI systems, where the 'hardware' supporting their artificial
intelligence is social rather than electronic" (p. 1221). Also the
responsibility-gap machinery, the operating-licence proposal, and Winfield &
Jirotka's "ethical black box" — the auditable record arriving from the
group-agency side. Honest limit: List concedes (fn. 38) that the machine leg has
no impossibility theorem under it.

#### Stage V — The handover and its critique

**25. `beer-vsm-provenance.pdf` ★★★**
The same-object claim as a scientific claim, with the metaphor reading refused:
the rules apply to a person and to the State "not by way of analogy." System
Five as identity closure.

**26. `medina-2006-cybersyn-article.pdf` ★★** — **NEW, and it supplies the
directory's best single scene:** Beer reaches System 5, "the place in the model
Beer had reserved for Allende himself," and Allende answers "At last, *el
pueblo*" (pp. 572–73). An officeholder looking at the seat drawn for him and
declining to identify it with himself. Read the failure too: designed as
devolution, run as technocracy; the Opsroom never became operational.

**27. `ulrich-1981-critique-cybernetic-reason.pdf` ★★★ ⚠** — **NEW, and the
objection the cluster was missing.** Three moves matter. (i) A viable system
"is not necessarily self-responsible with regard to the purposes it serves" —
the persona persists, the purposes belong to whoever fed it. (ii) The one aimed
squarely at the forward edge: "The syntactic aspect of communication processes
can be handled by information processing machines; the semantic and pragmatic
aspects only by people" (p. 35) — i.e. showing syntactic work is not
reason-giving, and a machine's audit trail may be the wrong kind of record
entirely. (iii) The guarantor analysis: planning "free from the arbitrariness of
the human will" makes disaster "like an act of God, which one must accept" — the
accountability sink derived twenty-odd years before Davies observed it. Also
catches Beer asserting a standing unelected office out of competence alone ("we
are responsible because cybernetics … is our profession," p. 56).
*Read immediately after, in dialogue with Ulrich's (ii):*
`pettit-2001-deliberative-discursive-dilemma.pdf` ★★, §4 — conversability, and
the sentence that ties this directory together: "The group is the entity that
continues over time despite changes in its membership, and that fact alone makes
it the salient agency that we should want to be contestable and conversable."

**28. `davies-2024-unaccountability-machine-publisher-preview.pdf` ★★★ ⚠** —
**NEW, and it supersedes the two Davies essays on the mechanism.** The
fundamental law of accountability (p. 17): "the extent to which you are able to
change a decision is precisely the extent to which you can be accountable for
it, and vice versa," with the sink defined as delegation to a rule book. **This
is the hardest objection in the directory to the whole device**, because it says
rotation and depersonalisation are not merely a way of keeping the individual
out — they are the canonical technique for making sure nobody is answerable, and
an auditable record that cannot be overridden is a better-documented sink rather
than a cure. Caputo's objection is answered by the record; Davies's law is not.
`davies-2025-…` ★★ stays for the OA-quotable version and the corporations-as-AI
framing.

#### Stage VI — Machine counsel: what showing the work would have to mean

**29. `mashaw-2001-small-things-like-reasons-fordham.pdf` ★★ ⚠** — **NEW, and read
before Stack, as Stack himself does.** The structural claim: legislatures
legitimate by will, courts by their capacity to settle, and only administration
has neither recourse — "The President made me do it" *delegitimates*
administrative action. If reason-giving is an office's sole legitimating
resource, then an instantiation that can show its work is not an improvement to
that office, it is the whole of it. Then the objection: showing your work is not
the same as being legitimate — the administrative state has overcapacity for
truth claims and undercapacity for claims of right and authenticity, and its
reasons "fail to attend to the range of reasons that we care about" (p. 29).

**30. `stack-2007-chenery.pdf` ★★★** — **NEW, and the most direct formalisation
of the thesis in the whole directory.** "The Chenery principle makes the
validity of agency action depend upon the validity of contemporaneous agency
reason-giving" (p. 956) — the record *is* the validity condition, not evidence
of it. Two beats land exactly: post-hoc rationalisation is forbidden precisely so
the instantiation cannot be filled in afterwards by whoever is convenient; and
reasons supplied by counsel do not count, because they were not embraced by the
accountable officeholder "in the first instance." Part V extends it to the
President. Caveat: the Chevron-conditioning architecture must be re-derived
post-*Loper Bright*.
*Depth underneath:* `schauer-1995-giving-reasons.pdf` ★★ — giving a reason
commits the giver to a generality beyond the instant case.

**31. `lazar-2024-democratic-duties-of-explanation.pdf` ★★★ ⚠**
Publicity as partly constitutive of legitimacy, owed to the community that
authorised the power. ⚠ because §VI pre-empts the comparative move ("humans
can't explain either") — engage it rather than route around it. Read against
stop 16: *Tidewater* is the strongest datum that the comparative move is not
merely a debating point.

**32. `engstrom-2020-government-by-algorithm.pdf` ★★★ ⚠** — **NEW, and the fact
base the forward edge has been missing.** 45% of 142 federal agencies, 12% of
uses technically sophisticated, 53% built in-house. Two payloads pull opposite
ways and both belong: the objection in operational form — many advanced tools
are "not, by their structure, fully explainable" (p. 7), so showing work is an
architectural property and not a promise; and the affirmative case at pp. 76–77,
made by administrative lawyers rather than enthusiasts — because algorithms
formalise priorities currently dispersed across unreviewable human judgment,
algorithmic enforcement may end up *more* transparent than the status quo and
may supply the reviewable focal point *Heckler v. Chaney* says does not exist.
Also names the "decisional shell" (p. 85), which is the office/occupant split as
it is actually being built.

**33. Adoption: the officeholder will not take the counsel.**
- `bonaccio-dalal-2006-advice-taking.pdf` ★★ — **NEW, and read first**, because
  the other two are measured against its baseline: a token 20–30% shift toward
  advice that should be weighted at 50%. Its independent payload is one sentence
  — advice is sought partly "to share accountability for the outcome," which is
  a motive the algorithm literature drops and which *Chenery* exists to block.
- `dietvorst-2015-algorithm-aversion.pdf` ★★ ⚠ — **NEW.** People abandon an
  algorithm faster than a human for the same error; the authors themselves flag
  that transparency may make adoption *less* likely if it makes error obvious.
  Table 7 is the interesting page: participants rate the model better on
  consistency, humans better on detecting exceptions and treating each case
  individually — Mashaw's authenticity complaint arriving from an unrelated
  literature.
- `logg-2019-algorithm-appreciation.pdf` ★★ ⚠ — **NEW, and the harder obstacle.**
  Lay people *appreciate* algorithmic advice; the 70 national-security
  professionals who forecast for a living discount it and lose accuracy for
  doing so. The people who staff offices are precisely the population that will
  not take machine counsel — and this needs no triggering error at all.
