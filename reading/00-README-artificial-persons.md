# Artificial Persons — reading list

The lineage behind the counsel arc's two newest moves (V.3a(e) and V.3c's
response to Teles, both 2026-08-06): **god, king, corporation, and state are the
same object — a collective intelligence personified, with humans as the
instantiation medium.** The machine version is not a new kind of thing; it is
the first instantiation that can show its work.

Two theses this directory supplies the lineage for, both Matthew's, both
verbatim in `outline-cosmic-ac.md`:

1. **The tulpa thesis.** The United States president "is not exactly a sovereign
   individual but is expected to be a kind of **tulpa for the leader of the
   United States**," and rotation between humans is "a way to avoid too much of
   the individual leaking into the instantiation." Term limits as
   regularisation.
2. **The shared-lie thesis.** One purpose of the belief in "some orientation
   towards the good or higher power directly watching you as an individual" is
   "to make up for the fact that without the lie, the things that are like that
   are **too hard to see for most people**" — and machine counsel "is a literal
   instantiation of those things that is **legible to everyone at any competence
   level**."

Sibling directories: `governance-and-state-capacity/` (the institutional record
of counsel), `democratization-of-counsel/` (the normative argument),
`the-trustworthy-firm/` (the household-side deployment). `notes/` is the shared
repo-versioned notes directory.

## The five clusters

**1. The doctrine: persons that are not people.** Kantorowicz, *The King's Two
Bodies* (Princeton, 1957) — the medieval jurists' *body natural* vs *body
politic*, the office-persona that "never dies" while humans rotate through it;
Hobbes, *Leviathan* (1651), ch. 16 "Of Persons, Authors, and Things Personated"
— the sovereign "beareth the person" of the commonwealth, the state as
"artificial man"; the corporate-personhood line of law (Blackstone on
corporations; *Dartmouth College v. Woodward*, 1819) — the "old line from law"
Teles reaches for when he calls the corporation an artificial intelligence.
This cluster is what makes V.3c's response to Teles an inheritance rather than
a novelty.

**2. Group agency, made formal.** List & Pettit, *Group Agency* (OUP, 2011) —
the analytic case that group agents are real agents with attitudes not
reducible to their members'; French, "The Corporation as a Moral Person" (*Am.
Phil. Q.* 16(3), 1979) — the moral-responsibility version. The discursive
dilemma is the formal proof that the collective's judgment is not any member's
judgment — the tulpa result, as a theorem.

**3. Cybernetics and the handover.** Beer's Viable System Model (the firm as a
designed nervous system; Cybersyn as the socialist-calculation debate built in
hardware — ties to the Hayek-criterion directory); Davies, *The Unaccountability
Machine* (2024) — accountability sinks, the objection carried in I.2 and V.3c;
Runciman, *The Handover* (2023) — states and corporations as the AIs we already
built and handed control to, the closest recent book-length statement of I.2's
premise.

**4. The watching god, measured.** The function of the personified collective
good: Plato's noble lie (*Republic* 414b–c); Voltaire's "necessary to invent
him" (1768); Durkheim, *Elementary Forms* (1912) — religion as society
worshipping itself, which is the shared-lie thesis in its canonical form;
Nietzsche, *Gay Science* §125 — the lie dies before anything replaces its
function. The modern measurement program: Norenzayan, *Big Gods* (2013);
Johnson & Krüger, "The Good of Wrath" (2004); Johnson, *God Is Watching You*
(2016); Purzycki et al., *Nature* 530 (2016) — moralistic, punishing, *knowing*
gods predict impartial cooperation with distant strangers. Read with Zuckerman,
*Society Without God* (2008): Denmark/Sweden shed the watcher without collapse
because high-trust legible institutions replaced it — the mechanism restated,
not a counterexample.

**Traps, recorded before they bite:** Whitehouse et al., *Nature* 2019
("Complex societies precede moralizing gods") is **RETRACTED (2021) — never
cite**. The lab watching-eyes priming effect **fails meta-analysis** (Northover
et al. 2017) — lean on the cross-cultural and historical work. Dostoevsky's "if
God does not exist, everything is permitted" **appears nowhere verbatim in
*Karamazov*** — the idea is Ivan's, the sentence is the translators'; quote
Nietzsche instead.

**5. Panopticon and its inversion.** Bentham, *Panopticon; or, The
Inspection-House* (1791) — the asymmetric special case: the inspector sees
without being seen; Foucault, *Discipline and Punish* (1975) — the
generalisation; then the published forms of Matthew's response (the worry
requires a power imbalance, which is what the solution addresses): Brin, *The
Transparent Society* (1998) — transparency benign when reciprocal; Mann, Nolan
& Wellman, "Sousveillance" (*Surveillance & Society* 1(3), 2003) — seeing from
below. The design point to keep honest: symmetry is a property that must be
defended, not a law of the technology.

## The reading path — the argument in order

### Stage I — The claim: god, king, corporation and state are one object

**1. `durkheim-1912-elementary-forms.pdf` ★★★**
Start here, not with Hobbes, because Durkheim states the unifying claim as a published sociological thesis rather than an author's invention: the god of the clan *is* the clan, personified. Read Bk. II ch. VII §§I–II and the Conclusion only. §II's reciprocity passage ([Pg 347] — "the sacred beings, though superior to men, can live only in the human consciousness") is the instantiation-medium half of the tulpa structure, in 1912.

**2. `hobbes-1651-leviathan.pdf` ★★★**
The same object as engineering. The Introduction's "Artificiall Man" anatomy already assigns counsellors the role of memory; ch. 16 gives the tulpa thesis its formal statement ("it is the Unity of the Representer, not the Unity of the Represented, that maketh the Person One"); ch. 19 names the rotation problem as "Artificiall Eternity." Then read **ch. 25, "Of Counsell"** — the unexpected find of the batch, and effectively a specification for machine counsel: counsellors with "their particular ends" are the defect, and "Rash, And Unevident Inferences ... Are Repugnant To The Office Of A Counsellour" is show-your-work as a duty of office.

**3. `dartmouth-v-woodward-1819.pdf` ★★★**
The doctrine alive and still controlling. Marshall at 636 makes rotation the *point* of the device — "a perpetual succession of many persons are considered as the same, and may act as a single individual" — and Story at 667 supplies the instantiation-medium sentence outright. This is what lets the paper stand on law instead of asserting the identity as metaphor. Keep Marshall's deflationary limit in view: the corporation is a creature of charter, explicitly *not* a participant in civil government.

**4. `shortall-2023-kings-two-bodies-secularization.pdf` ★★★ [OBJECTION]**
Read immediately after 3, while the genealogy is still fresh. Gives a reliable summary of the king's two bodies (body politic outranking body natural; Plowden's "as to this Body the King never dies"), then argues that theological genealogies prove less than they are used to prove: "even if a modern political concept can be shown to derive from theological origins, it does not follow that the concept continues to operate theologically in the present" (p. 806). **This is the best-credentialled published statement of why shared descent is not identity**, and carrying it costs the paper nothing, because the real load is borne by the mechanism (personified office, rotating medium), not by the genealogy. Print it; do not route around it.

### Stage II — The mechanism: how the office keeps the individual out

**5. `french-1979-corporation-moral-person.pdf` ★★★**
The mechanism the tulpa thesis needs, published in 1979 so the paper inherits rather than invents. The CID Structure — chart plus recognition rules — exists to launder the individual out of the collective act: personal reasons are "diluted by the subordination of individual inputs to peer group input," and the corporation still has reasons even when a voter has none ("X did not join the cartel, Gulf Oil Corporation joined the cartel," p. 214). This is the direct answer to Teles: the discretion did not vanish when you looked for the human holding it; **it was never located in a human.** Cite the CID mechanism, not the contested moral-personhood conclusion. Note for Stage III: the CID Structure is a redescription licence, not a record — it explains that the corporation had reasons without ever exhibiting them.

**6. `list-2018-group-agent-what-is-it-like.pdf` ★★★ [OBJECTION]**
The analytic form of "a collective intelligence personified": group awareness is real on functionalist grounds, and — the show-your-work point — third-personally analysable in a way human awareness is not. Then the objection, and it is sharp: no phenomenal consciousness, therefore only *derivative* rights and "especially strict checks and controls" (p. 37). Applied to machine counsel that cuts directly against granting the instantiation standing of its own. It also carries its own contest honestly (Schwitzgebel, Huebner, Briggs) and is conditional on IIT, which List himself flags as controversial. **Caveat for planning: this is not the discursive-dilemma paper — List sets the aggregation machinery aside at p. 8.** See Gaps.

**7. `beer-vsm-provenance.pdf` ★★★**
The same-object claim made as a scientific claim, with the metaphor reading explicitly refused: the rules "apply to an organism such as a human being, or to an organization consisting of human beings such as the State ... not by way of analogy." Beer pre-empts the free-will objection — "What matters is the functioning of an element ... not the identity of the element itself" — and then, at p. 10, states the tulpa thesis about a sitting head of state: Allende correcting him that System Five was not the president but the people, with the coup named as the failure mode. Also the cleanest published account of the polity as a *designed* nervous system, which is what machine counsel proposes to redesign.

**8. `plato-republic-jowett.pdf` ★★ *(skippable)***
Read 416d–417b here, not with the noble lie: guardians may hold no private property, no closed house, no gold, on pain of becoming "housekeepers and husbandmen instead of guardians" — an office designed to suppress its occupant, twenty-three centuries before term limits. The 414b material belongs to Stage III; see Side tracks.

**9. `runciman-corporations-ai-essay.pdf` ★★ *(skippable)* [OBJECTION]**
The bridge into the show-your-work claim, in four quotable sentences: corporations are "another form of artificial thinking machine," and three hundred years of law has produced "little progress in understanding what goes on inside the black box of the corporate mind." The tulpa thesis in journalistic form — "Kalanick is not Uber — he goes, but the business goes on." Skippable because French and List make every substantive claim more rigorously; keep it as the OA verification anchor for the Runciman/*Handover* line the outline already flags. Do not print the Thurlow quotation without independent verification — it is Runciman's paraphrase of an apocryphal line.

### Stage III — Thesis 2: the shared lie, and what replaces it

**10. `voltaire-1769-epitre-trois-imposteurs.pdf` ★★★**
The founding text, in the original, with a real citation (Garnier t. 10, pp. 402–405) instead of the second-hand English everyone else prints. The famous line is there, but the more valuable passage is the one nobody quotes: "Rois, si vous m'opprimez ... Mon vengeur est au ciel : apprenez à trembler." **The watching god aimed upward at the powerful — the paper's symmetry answer already present in 1769.** Correct the filename's date to 1769; supply your own translation or cite a published one.

**11. `nietzsche-gay-science-125-madman.pdf` ★★★**
The fear thesis 2 answers, in canonical form — and note its actual shape: loss of *reference frame*, not of comfort ("Is there still an above and below?"). That maps onto the claim that the orientation structures were always there and the god-belief was the legible proxy. §125 at pp. 167–169; **also read §343 at pp. 275–276**, which puts Nietzsche on the answering side rather than only the fearing side. Pairs directly with Durkheim's "the old gods are growing old or already dead, and others are not yet born." Common's 1910 wording, not Kaufmann's — do not blend.

**12. `norenzayan-shariff-2008-religious-prosociality.pdf` ★★★ [OBJECTION]**
The hinge. Mechanism stated crisply — salient moralistic deities make "an objectively anonymous situation ... reputationally relevant" — and then the substitution claim the paper builds on: courts, policing and contract enforcement "changed the course of human prosociality," with the secular-but-cooperative Northern European datapoint attached. Machine counsel becomes the next member of a series the literature already named. **The objection is inside the same paper:** religious prosociality is "a bounded phenomenon," ingroup-scoped, and "[t]he same mechanisms involved in ingroup altruism may also facilitate outgroup antagonism." Quote that against the thesis, not around it.

**13. `purzycki-2016-moralistic-gods.pdf` ★★★**
The measurement of the exact attribute thesis 2 names — a god who knows your *thoughts*, watching you as an individual. n=591 across eight communities, 35,400 allocations; the local-gods null (ORs 0.96–1.01) is the strongest available answer to omitted-variable objections. Four flags travel with it: the authors call it correlational; their own priming manipulation had no effect; the dependent variable is impartiality toward distant *co-religionists*, i.e. ingroup expansion; Templeton-funded via CERC. Check the post-2016 re-analyses before printing the 4.8×/5.3× figures.

**14. `northover-2017-watching-eyes-meta.pdf` ★★★ [OBJECTION]**
**The batch's hardest counter, and it belongs on the spine.** Two random-effects meta-analyses of artificial surveillance cues on generosity: g = 0.03, CI −0.08 to 0.13 (k=26); log-OR 0.16, CI −0.04 to 0.35 (k=27). This removes the analogical bridge that Shariff & Norenzayan lean on when they argue from eyespots to supernatural watchers, and the moderator chaos they document (Ekström vs Bateson on crowd density, in flat contradiction) is directly usable in the panopticon section. It cuts both ways and the paper should say so: it weakens the claim that a machine watcher automatically produces orientation-toward-good, and it equally weakens the panopticon fear that a symbolic observer coerces behaviour. Scope it honestly — artificial cues, generosity only.

**15. `shariff-norenzayan-2007-god-is-watching.pdf` ★★ *(skippable)***
Read only if the dollar figures are load-bearing. Study 2's three-arm design is the head-to-head the thesis wants: secular prime $4.44 vs God prime $4.56 vs control $2.56 — a reminder of a *visible, human-built* accountability structure doing the same work as the invisible watcher. Also credits the prior claim to Voltaire and Rousseau rather than claiming it. n=50 and n=75, reported d = 1.07 and 0.71, prep, and the exact paradigm Northover meta-analyses to zero. Never print without its contest.

**16. `johnson-kruger-2004-good-of-wrath.pdf` ★★ *(skippable)***
Theory, no new data: why a *watching* god specifically is the functional object — punishment is costly, so enforcement is a second-order public good with its own free-riders, which an omniscient punisher dissolves at zero cost to any believer. Two details earn their keep: surveillance extending to thoughts (Mt. 5:28), which is the panopticon's internalisation claim reached independently, and their framing of people who "subjugate themselves to an illusory threat without 'knowing' it" — the shared lie as cost-benefit, and a clean foil for the point that machine counsel does the same work without the illusion. Take the Voltaire line from Voltaire, not from their note 64.

**17. `zuckerman-2009-atheism-secularity-wellbeing.pdf` ★★ *(skippable)* [OBJECTION]**
Cuts at thesis 2's premise rather than its conclusion: on this evidence, removing the belief leaves no gap needing replacement — secular democracies lead happiness indices and sit at the bottom of homicide rates, and within the US the most religious states are the most violent. It equally refutes the specific fear the paper answers, that freer information yields nihilism. Zuckerman disciplines himself in print (the suicide exception, conceded twice; "correlation is not causation"; atheistic and religious dictatorships both attributed to the dictatorship term), which makes this a contest printable without strawmanning. Every number is borrowed — cite Paul 2005, Norris & Inglehart 2004 directly, and find the published *Sociology Compass* version.

### Stage IV — The panopticon worry and the symmetry answer

**18. `bentham-1791-panopticon.pdf` ★★★**
Go to the primary source, not to Bentham-through-Foucault, for three passages. Letter I p. 3: what matters is not that the inmate is watched but that he cannot satisfy himself he is not. Letter VI p. 28: "the *apparent omnipresence* of the Inspector (if divines will allow me the expression)" — the cluster-4/cluster-5 bridge, the watching god secularised into architecture, with the theological analogy conceded by the designer. Letter VI p. 33: the doors "thrown wide open ... the great *open committee* of the tribunal of the world" — **the symmetry answer as an inheritance from the panopticon's own author.** Image-only scan; transcribe off rasterised pages, cite by Letter and book page.

**19. `foucault-panopticism-chapter.pdf` ★★★ [OBJECTION]**
Three jobs. The worry in its citable form ("He is seen, but he does not see; he is the object of information, never a subject in communication") — state it in Foucault's words before answering it. Then the sharpest objection in the batch: the mechanism "automatizes and disindividualizes power," "it does not matter who exercises power," and the schema's "vocation was to become a generalized function." **If power inheres in the arrangement rather than in the occupant of the tower, universal watching does not dissolve the mechanism — it universalises it.** Thesis 2's symmetry answer must meet that. Third, Foucault himself records Bentham's reciprocity provision (the transparent-building sentence), so the answer can be sourced through the canonical critic. No page numbers in this reprint; quote Bentham from Bentham.

**20. `mann-nolan-wellman-2003-sousveillance.pdf` ★★★ [OBJECTION]**
The only work here that field-tests the symmetry answer, and it argues against its own proposal: "Universal surveillance/sousveillance may, in the end, only serve the ends of the existing dominant power structure" (p. 347). Three findings the paper needs: asymmetry is enforced *socially* — organisational surveillers object to being recorded back, and objection rises with the density of their own cameras; the "will not / may not / cannot" hierarchy, where objections collapse once the sousveiller is visibly acting under external authority, which is uncomfortable for any design hoping symmetry emerges from individual capability; and the accountability sink observed in the field, responsibility "defus[ing] into generalized deferrals to the way things are" (p. 346). Five art performances in one Canadian shopping district — mechanism and objection, never measurement.

**21. `brin-1996-transparent-society-essay.pdf` ★★ *(skippable)***
Close with the priority date. City Two contains the design point in one clause — cameras banned indoors "except Police Headquarters!", where any citizen may watch the control room — i.e. the powerful become the most-watched, published 1996. Skippable because the argument survives on Bentham + Foucault + Mann; its value is attribution, not soundness. Advocacy with no honest limit, and its 1996 press numbers are unsourced. Cite the 1998 book for the argument, this essay for the date and the wording, and say which.

## Side tracks

**A. The priming programme and its collapse.** `shariff-norenzayan-2007` + `johnson-kruger-2004`, read against `northover-2017`. A self-contained bundle: mechanism proposal, the headline experiment, and the meta-analysis that removes its analogical support. Read all three together or none — printing any one alone misrepresents the state of the evidence. Only the meta-analysis is on the spine.

**B. The popular compressions.** `runciman-corporations-ai-essay` + `brin-1996-transparent-society-essay`. Both are OA short forms of books on the purchase list (*The Handover*; *The Transparent Society*), both supply a quotable framing sentence, neither supplies evidence. Their real function is verification anchoring for outline claims currently carrying VERIFY flags. Both are browser-rendered from live pages — no page numbers may be cited from either file.

**C. Secularity without a gap.** `zuckerman-2009` alone. A coherent off-spine challenge to thesis 2's *premise* rather than its argument, and the place to go if a reviewer asks what happens when the shared lie simply leaves. Follow its citations out rather than quoting its numbers.

**D. Ancient office design.** `plato-republic-jowett` 416d–417b, read next to Dartmouth and Beer rather than with the noble-lie material. The guardians' property ban is the oldest statement in the directory of an office built to suppress the person filling it. The 414b passage is the shared-lie thesis's named ancestor but functions as its **foil** — Plato's fiction is top-down, total, and must not be inspected, which is the exact inversion of a counsel that shows its work. Quote the framing sentence only, never the myth's autochthony-and-caste content, and never call it the "noble lie" while quoting Jowett, whose text reads "royal lie."

## Gaps the path-builder surfaced

Excluding what the purchase list already covers (Kantorowicz 1957, List & Pettit 2011, Norenzayan 2013, Runciman 2023, Davies 2024, Brin 1998, Foucault 1975, Zuckerman 2008):

1. **The discursive dilemma is missing outright.** The README's cluster-2 framing expects "the tulpa result as a theorem," and List 2018 explicitly declines to supply it, pointing to List & Pettit 2011 — which is on the purchase list and unavailable. The judgment-aggregation result has OA primary literature predating the book (the List & Pettit and Pettit journal papers of 2001–02); acquire one of those rather than waiting on the monograph. Without it, the claim that a collective's reasoning is structurally not its members' reasoning rests on French's redescription licence alone.

2. **Cybersyn as the socialist-calculation debate in hardware is unsourced.** It is not in the Beer file — Chile appears only as provenance. Beer's own reference list points at Espejo (1980a) for the operations-room and economic-calculation material; that is the acquisition.

3. **The technocratic-control critique of the VSM is named but absent.** Beer's references include his reply to Ulrich's "Critique of pure cybernetic reason: the Chilean experiment with cybernetics." A paper proposing machine counsel in governance must carry that critique rather than cite Beer clean. Get Ulrich's original *and* Beer's reply.

4. **Nothing measures rotation.** Term limits as regularisation is thesis 1's operative claim, and the directory supports it only doctrinally (Dartmouth's perpetual succession, Hobbes's artificial eternity). There is no empirical work here on whether rotation actually prevents the individual leaking into the office — the legislative term-limits literature, or the executive-succession literature, is a real hole.

5. **Teles is answered at second hand.** The objection that automating cognition "pulls out where the actual discretion and judgment are" is the argument Stage II exists to meet, and the source is not in the directory. Acquire the Teles piece so the rebuttal quotes it accurately.

6. **Nothing on machine counsel as practised.** No work here on advice-taking, algorithmic aversion, or automated decision support inside bureaucracies. The whole applied half of both theses currently rests on Hobbes ch. 25 as its only specification.

7. **"Shows its work" has no modern legal counterpart.** Hobbes's duty to propound advice "in such forme of speech, as may make the truth most evidently appear" is the directory's only account of what showing work consists of. The administrative-law reason-giving literature — duties to state grounds, reviewability of unexplained decisions — is the natural companion and is entirely absent. Note also that French's CID Structure is explicitly *not* a record, which sharpens the need.

8. **The symmetry answer has anecdote, not measurement.** Mann is five performances; Brin is advocacy. Nothing quantitative on whether transparency directed at the powerful changes their conduct (FOIA/open-government effect studies, body-camera trials). This is the most consequential empirical gap for the panopticon-inversion argument, because Foucault's disindividualised-power objection cannot be answered by design intent alone.

9. **The post-2016 re-analyses of the CERC dataset.** Flagged in the Purzycki notes and not on disk; the 4.8×/5.3× figures should not be printed until they are read.

10. **Translation infrastructure.** Voltaire is French-only with no published English translation in the directory, and Nietzsche is Common 1910 where most readers will expect Kaufmann. Acquire a citable English Voltaire and a modern paginated *Gay Science* so §125/§343 can be cross-referenced.

11. **Housekeeping, not scholarship:** the Dartmouth scan is dirty OCR of a whole U.S. Reports volume and the Bentham has no text layer at all — every quotation from either must be proofed against the page image before it reaches print.

## What the ranking changed (recorded because each corrects or extends the outline)

**1. Hobbes ch. 25 is a specification for machine counsel, found by reading
past the assignment.** The Introduction already assigns counsellors the role of
memory in the artificial man; ch. 25 then states the two defects machine counsel
removes, as *duties of the office of counsellour*, in 1651: a natural man's
senses "work upon him without passion, or interest of their own; whereas they
that give Counsell to the Representative person of a Common-wealth, may have,
and have often their particular ends" — the counsellor-without-private-ends
requirement; and "Rash, And Unevident Inferences … Are Repugnant To The Office
Of A Counsellour" — **show-your-work as a duty of office, three centuries before
the paper asked for it.** V.3c should quote this.

**2. The symmetry answer has a deeper inheritance than Brin.** It is in
