# The Shared Lie — reading list

The lineage behind **the shared-lie thesis** (V.3a(e), Matthew, 2026-08-06,
verbatim in `outline-cosmic-ac.md`): one purpose of the belief in "some
orientation towards the good or higher power directly watching you as an
individual" is "to make up for the fact that without the lie, the things that
are like that are **too hard to see for most people**" — and machine counsel
"is a literal instantiation of those things that is **legible to everyone at
any competence level**." The fear it answers: freer information produces
nihilism. And the panopticon worry it must survive, with Matthew's response
(2026-08-06): ⟨M⟩ the worry **requires a power imbalance — which is what the
solution is trying to address**. ⟨/M⟩

**The unifying claim (stated in both directories; Durkheim is the hinge):**
god, king, corporation, and state are the same object — a collective
intelligence personified, with humans as the instantiation medium. The machine
version is the first instantiation that can show its work.

Split 2026-08-07 from `artificial-persons/` at Matthew's direction (that
directory holds the tulpa thesis: doctrine, group agency, cybernetics). This
one holds the experimental-and-meta-analytic half: read it by weighing
evidence, not by following proofs. `notes/` is shared.

## The three clusters

**1. The founding texts.** Plato's noble lie (*Republic* 414b–c — Jowett reads
"royal lie"; and 416d–417b, the guardians' property ban, the oldest office
designed to suppress its occupant); Voltaire's Épître (1769) — "il faudrait
l'inventer," **and the un-quoted passage that is the symmetry answer in 1769:
"Rois, si vous m'opprimez … Mon vengeur est au ciel: apprenez à trembler"** —
the watching god aimed upward at the powerful; Nietzsche, *Gay Science* §125
(the fear in canonical form: loss of reference frame, "Is there still an above
and below?") **and §343** (Nietzsche on the answering side); Durkheim — **the
hinge work with `artificial-persons/`**: the god of the clan *is* the clan
personified, and "the sacred beings, though superior to men, can live only in
the human consciousness."

**2. The watching god, measured — and the corrections.** Norenzayan & Shariff
2008 (the mechanism: salient moralistic deities make anonymity "reputationally
relevant"; the substitution claim — courts, police, contracts "changed the
course of human prosociality"); Purzycki 2016 (a god who knows your *thoughts*;
local-gods null); Shariff & Norenzayan 2007 (secular prime $4.44 ≈ God prime
$4.56 vs control $2.56 — a *visible human-built accountability structure doing
the same work*); Johnson & Krüger 2004 (the omniscient punisher dissolves the
second-order free-rider problem); Northover 2017 (**the meta-analytic zero** on
artificial watching cues); Zuckerman 2009 (secular societies without collapse —
the premise contested honestly). Incoming from the citation graph: the CERC
re-analyses (Bendixen 2023, Lightner 2023 — **read before printing the
4.8×/5.3× figures**), Beheim 2021 (the analysis that forced the Whitehouse
retraction — cite this, never the headline), the priming-replication record
(van Elk 2015, Gomes & McCullough 2015, the Many-Analysts project), Laurin 2012
(supernatural↔institutional monitor substitution, measured), White 2019 (karma:
moral monitoring without a personified god), Watts 2015, Lang 2019
(impartiality inside, hostility outside), and Fitouchi 2025 — **the strongest
live competitor framing**: religions as folk technologies of horizontal mutual
policing, not vertical watching.

**3. The panopticon, and its inversion.** Bentham 1791 primary — Letter I (the
inmate cannot satisfy himself he is *not* watched), Letter VI ("the *apparent
omnipresence* of the Inspector (if divines will allow me the expression)" —
the watching god secularised into architecture, conceded by the designer; and
the doors "thrown wide open … the great *open committee* of the tribunal of the
world" — **the symmetry answer inherited from the panopticon's own author**);
Foucault (the worry citable — "He is seen, but he does not see" — and the
sharpest objection to the symmetry answer: power "automatizes and
disindividualizes"; **if power inheres in the arrangement, universal watching
universalises the mechanism**); Mann-Nolan-Wellman 2003 (sousveillance
field-tested, arguing against its own proposal: asymmetry is *socially*
enforced); Brin's Wired 4.12 essay (the priority date for
the-powerful-become-the-most-watched, 1996).

## The reading path — the shared-lie argument in order
(Entries and numbering from the joint pass.)

**1. `durkheim-1912-elementary-forms.pdf` ★★★**
Start here, not with Hobbes, because Durkheim states the unifying claim as a published sociological thesis rather than an author's invention: the god of the clan *is* the clan, personified. Read Bk. II ch. VII §§I–II and the Conclusion only. §II's reciprocity passage ([Pg 347] — "the sacred beings, though superior to men, can live only in the human consciousness") is the instantiation-medium half of the tulpa structure, in 1912.


### Stage III — Thesis 2: the shared lie, and what replaces it

**10. `voltaire-1769-epitre-trois-imposteurs.pdf` ★★★**
The founding text, in the original, with a real citation (Garnier t. 10, pp. 402–405) instead of the second-hand English everyone else prints. The famous line is there, but the more valuable passage is the one nobody quotes: "Rois, si vous m'opprimez ... Mon vengeur est au ciel : apprenez à trembler." **The watching god aimed upward at the powerful — the paper's symmetry answer already present in 1769.** Correct the filename's date to 1769; supply your own translation or cite a published one.

**11. `nietzsche-gay-science-125-madman.pdf` ★★★**
The fear thesis 2 answers, in canonical form — and note its actual shape: loss of *reference frame*, not of comfort ("Is there still an above and below?"). That maps onto the claim that the orientation structures were always there and the god-belief was the legible proxy. §125 at pp. 167–169; **also read §343 at pp. 275–276**, which puts Nietzsche on the answering side rather than only the fearing side. Pairs directly with Durkheim's "the old gods are growing old or already dead, and others are not yet born." Common's 1910 wording, not Kaufmann's — do not blend.

**12. `norenzayan-shariff-2008-religious-prosociality.pdf` ★★★ [OBJECTION]**
The hinge. Mechanism stated crisply — salient moralistic deities make "an objectively anonymous situation ... reputationally relevant" — and then the substitution claim the paper builds on: courts, policing and contract enforcement "changed the course of human prosociality," with the secular-but-cooperative Northern European datapoint attached. Machine counsel becomes the next member of a series the literature already named. **The objection is inside the same paper:** religious prosociality is "a bounded phenomenon," ingroup-scoped, and "[t]he same mechanisms involved in ingroup altruism may also facilitate outgroup antagonism." Quote that against the thesis, not around it.

**13. `purzycki-2016-moralistic-gods.pdf` ★★★**
The measurement of the exact attribute thesis 2 names — a god who knows your *thoughts*, watching you as an individual. n=591 across eight communities, 35,400 allocations; the local-gods null (ORs 0.96–1.01) is the strongest available answer to omitted-variable objections. Four flags travel with it: the authors call it correlational; their own priming manipulation had no effect; the dependent variable is impartiality toward distant *co-religionists*, i.e. ingroup expansion; Templeton-funded via CERC. Check the post-2016 re-analyses before printing the 4.8×/5.3× figures.

**14. `northover-2017-watching-eyes-meta.pdf` ★★★ [OBJECTION]**
**The batch's hardest counter, and it belongs on the spine.** Two random-effects meta-analyses of artificial surveillance cues on generosity: g = 0.03, CI −0.08 to 0.13 (k=26); log-OR 0.16, CI −0.04 to 0.35 (k=27). This removes the analogical bridge that Shariff & Norenzayan lean on when they argue from eyespots to supernatural watchers, and the moderator chaos they document (Ekström vs Bateson on crowd density, in flat contradiction) is directly usable in the panopticon section. It cuts both ways and the paper should say so: it weakens the claim that a machine watcher automatically produces orientation-toward-good, and it equally weakens the panopticon fear that a symbolic observer coerces behaviour. Scope it honestly — artificial cues, generosity only.

**15. `shariff-norenzayan-2007-god-is-watching.pdf` ★★ *(skippable)***
Read only if the dollar figures are load-bearing. Study 2's three-arm design is the head-to-head the thesis wants: secular prime $4.44 vs God prime $4.56 vs control $2.56 — a reminder of a *visible, human-built* accountability structure doing the same work as the invisible watcher. Also credits the prior claim to Voltaire and Rousseau rather than claiming it. n=50 and n=75, reported d = 1.07 and 0.71, prep, and the exact paradigm Northover meta-analyses to zero. Never print without its contest.

**16. `johnson-kruger-2004-good-of-wrath.pdf` ★★ *(skippable)***
Theory, no new data: why a *watching* god specifically is the functional object — punishment is costly, so enforcement is a second-order public good with its own free-riders, which an omniscient punisher dissolves at zero cost to any believer. Two details earn their keep: surveillance extending to thoughts (Mt. 5:28), which is the panopticon's internalisation claim reached independently, and their framing of people who "subjugate themselves to an illusory threat without 'knowing' it" — the shared lie as cost-benefit, and a clean foil for the point that machine counsel does the same work without the illusion. Take the Voltaire line from Voltaire, not from their note 64.

**17. `zuckerman-2009-atheism-secularity-wellbeing.pdf` ★★ *(skippable)* [OBJECTION]**
Cuts at thesis 2's premise rather than its conclusion: on this evidence, removing the belief leaves no gap needing replacement — secular democracies lead happiness indices and sit at the bottom of homicide rates, and within the US the most religious states are the most violent. It equally refutes the specific fear the paper answers, that freer information yields nihilism. Zuckerman disciplines himself in print (the suicide exception, conceded twice; "correlation is not causation"; atheistic and religious dictatorships both attributed to the dictatorship term), which makes this a contest printable without strawmanning. Every number is borrowed — cite Paul 2005, Norris & Inglehart 2004 directly, and find the published *Sociology Compass* version.

### Stage IV — The panopticon worry and the symmetry answer

**18. `bentham-1791-panopticon.pdf` ★★★**
Go to the primary source, not to Bentham-through-Foucault, for three passages. Letter I p. 3: what matters is not that the inmate is watched but that he cannot satisfy himself he is not. Letter VI p. 28: "the *apparent omnipresence* of the Inspector (if divines will allow me the expression)" — the cluster-4/cluster-5 bridge, the watching god secularised into architecture, with the theological analogy conceded by the designer. Letter VI p. 33: the doors "thrown wide open ... the great *open committee* of the tribunal of the world" — **the symmetry answer as an inheritance from the panopticon's own author.** Image-only scan; transcribe off rasterised pages, cite by Letter and book page.

**19. `foucault-panopticism-chapter.pdf` ★★★ [OBJECTION]**
Three jobs. The worry in its citable form ("He is seen, but he does not see; he is the object of information, never a subject in communication") — state it in Foucault's words before answering it. Then the sharpest objection in the batch: the mechanism "automatizes and disindividualizes power," "it does not matter who exercises power," and the schema's "vocation was to become a generalized function." **If power inheres in the arrangement rather than in the occupant of the tower, universal watching does not dissolve the mechanism — it universalises it.** Thesis 2's symmetry answer must meet that. Third, Foucault himself records Bentham's reciprocity provision (the transparent-building sentence), so the answer can be sourced through the canonical critic. No page numbers in this reprint; quote Bentham from Bentham.

**20. `mann-nolan-wellman-2003-sousveillance.pdf` ★★★ [OBJECTION]**
The only work here that field-tests the symmetry answer, and it argues against its own proposal: "Universal surveillance/sousveillance may, in the end, only serve the ends of the existing dominant power structure" (p. 347). Three findings the paper needs: asymmetry is enforced *socially* — organisational surveillers object to being recorded back, and objection rises with the density of their own cameras; the "will not / may not / cannot" hierarchy, where objections collapse once the sousveiller is visibly acting under external authority, which is uncomfortable for any design hoping symmetry emerges from individual capability; and the accountability sink observed in the field, responsibility "defus[ing] into generalized deferrals to the way things are" (p. 346). Five art performances in one Canadian shopping district — mechanism and objection, never measurement.

**21. `brin-1996-transparent-society-essay.pdf` ★★ *(skippable)***
Close with the priority date. City Two contains the design point in one clause — cameras banned indoors "except Police Headquarters!", where any citizen may watch the control room — i.e. the powerful become the most-watched, published 1996. Skippable because the argument survives on Bentham + Foucault + Mann; its value is attribution, not soundness. Advocacy with no honest limit, and its 1996 press numbers are unsourced. Cite the 1998 book for the argument, this essay for the date and the wording, and say which.

## Side tracks

**A. The priming programme and its collapse.** `shariff-norenzayan-2007` + `johnson-kruger-2004`, read against `northover-2017`. A self-contained bundle: mechanism proposal, the headline experiment, and the meta-analysis that removes its analogical support. Read all three together or none — printing any one alone misrepresents the state of the evidence. Only the meta-analysis is on the spine.


**C. Secularity without a gap.** `zuckerman-2009` alone. A coherent off-spine challenge to thesis 2's *premise* rather than its argument, and the place to go if a reviewer asks what happens when the shared lie simply leaves. Follow its citations out rather than quoting its numbers.


**D. Ancient office design.** `plato-republic-jowett` 416d–417b, read next to Dartmouth and Beer rather than with the noble-lie material. The guardians' property ban is the oldest statement in the directory of an office built to suppress the person filling it. The 414b passage is the shared-lie thesis's named ancestor but functions as its **foil** — Plato's fiction is top-down, total, and must not be inspected, which is the exact inversion of a counsel that shows its work. Quote the framing sentence only, never the myth's autochthony-and-caste content, and never call it the "noble lie" while quoting Jowett, whose text reads "royal lie."

This is the most consequential empirical gap for the panopticon-inversion argument, because Foucault's disindividualised-power objection cannot be answered by design intent alone.
