# Reading notes — Arrow 1969 (Matthew, 2026-07-29)

Matthew's find, verified verbatim against the copy on disk (p.2 of the JEC
document):

> "Given the existence of Pareto inefficiency in a free market equilibrium,
> there is a pressure in the market to overcome it by some sort of departure
> from the free market; i.e., some form of collective action. This need not be
> undertaken by the Government. I suggest that in fact there is a wide variety
> of social institutions, in particular generally accepted social norms of
> behavior, which serve in some means as compensation for failure or limitation
> of the market, though each in turn involves transaction costs of its own."

Placement: IV.3 (the bubble as the market's own departure-from-market), the
cure movement generally (the commons as pressure-produced collective action),
and the norms clause → I.2/V.3 (norms as market-failure compensations = the
economics twin of religion-as-engineered-preferences). Also note, same page:
"The creation of money is in many respects an example of a public good" —
adjacent to V.6's money thread, possibly useful.

Arrow's caveat in the same passage — each compensating institution "involves
transaction costs of its own" — is worth keeping when quoted: it is the honest
version (the commons is not free either; governance is its transaction cost,
which is what the limits material already says).

## Addendum (2026-07-30) — the Arrow clause
Matthew's dynamic corollary: the pressure Arrow describes, if denied benign
departures, discharges through the cheapest available channel — historically
including violent uprising. Anchors: Scheidel's *Great Leveler* (the Four
Horsemen as the violent tail); Acemoglu-Robinson QJE 2000 (franchise extension
under threat of revolution = the peaceful discharge; doi verified). Re-reads
the productive bubble as a benign self-assessed discharge (capital socialized
via crash rather than expropriation), and gives the commons its self-interest
form: the fair split as the surplus holders' cheapest long-run option.
