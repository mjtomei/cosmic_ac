## The verified goldmine (checked against the scan, p. 388, §3)

Section 3 is titled "Impossibility of decentralized spontaneous solution" and
contains, verbatim:
- "…the involved optimizing equations that an **omniscient calculating machine
  could theoretically solve if fed the postulated functions. No such machine
  now exists.**"
- "…it is in the selfish interest of each person to give **false signals**, to
  pretend to have less interest in a given collective consumption activity…"
- "In terms of **communication theory** and game terminology, each person is
  motivated to do the signalling of his tastes needed to define and reach the
  attainable-bliss point."
- "…political decision-making would still be necessary, but of a
  **computationally minimum type**."

Consequence: the founding paper of cluster A already contains the criterion's
seed — the machine conceded (computation), "if fed the postulated functions"
(bandwidth), false signals (elicitation). Hayek 1945, nine years earlier, held
bandwidth and drift. The two founding papers each state half the criterion, from
opposite politics. This is the frame V.5 should open with.

## Note 1 — no pure collective or private good; exclusivity has value
Literature: Buchanan 1965 (clubs — the continuum; Samuelson himself conceded
polar cases in his 1955 diagrammatic follow-up); Ostrom's 2×2 (excludability ×
subtractability); Leibenstein 1950 (bandwagon/snob/Veblen — utility depends on
others' allocations); Hirsch 1976 *Social Limits to Growth* (positional goods —
exclusivity valued per se). The "value of exclusivity" point maps onto the
paper's herd section: a moat is a positional good; part of what the current
equilibrium optimizes is *relative* standing, which is exactly what a commons
refuses to price.

## Note 2 — utility functions not fixed, can flip; meta-goods move the frontier;
## advertisers exploit the gap
Literature: Galbraith 1958 (dependence effect) vs Hayek 1961 "The Non Sequitur
of the 'Dependence Effect'" (the fun twist: Hayek's reply concedes wants are
learned and argues it doesn't matter — same author anchoring both clusters);
von Weizsäcker 1971 (welfare criteria when tastes change — the formal version of
"meta-goods change the Pareto frontier"); Stigler-Becker 1977 (the opposite
pole: stable deep preferences, advertising as information — any endogeneity
claim must answer it); Bowles 1998 JEL (the survey; institutions select
preferences); Becker-Murphy 1993 (ads as complements). The deep problem Matthew
is pointing at: **Pareto criteria are ill-defined under endogenous preferences**
— optimal by whose utility function, the pre- or post-allocation one? That is
not a nitpick; it is a known open wound in welfare economics.

## Note 3 — distributed search; phones realizing utility functions; religion and
## morality as engineered utility functions
The distributed-search point is Hayek 1945 restated with utility functions as
the dispersed knowledge — and the elicitation leg is its game-theoretic half
(Samuelson's false signals → Hurwicz IC → Clarke 1971 / Groves-Ledyard 1977,
the demand-revealing mechanisms that partially ANSWERED Samuelson's pessimism —
public-goods provision has incentive-compatible mechanisms, with known caveats:
budget imbalance, collusion fragility). Phones-as-utility-realizers = the
paper's IV.4 scheduler + V.4 devices, and the current platform exploitation of
exactly that capability is the covert version of I.3. Religion/morality as
engineered preferences: cultural-evolution literature (Henrich; Norenzayan's
*Big Gods* — prosocial religions as scaling technology; D.S. Wilson). Frank 1988
(*Passions Within Reason* — moral emotions as commitment devices) is already on
the books list.

## Note 4 — noise, nonmonotonicity, deception in the noise; clear signaling
## non-optimal
Literature: Crawford & Sobel 1982 (strategic information transmission — in cheap
talk, only coarse/partial-pooling equilibria survive; clear signaling is
literally non-optimal for the sender); the costly-signaling line (Spence, in the
collection's sibling directory). Maps onto the criterion's elicitation leg as
its game-on-top-of-the-game.

## Addendum 2 (2026-07-30) — surveillance pricing as the deployed extractive
## version of realized utility functions
Matthew's datapoint: per-user pricing in consumer apps (industry/regulatory
term: "surveillance pricing" — FTC 6(b) study, Jan 2025 press release verified;
economics: first-degree price discrimination). This is note 3's "currently
exploited by the companies managing the software" made concrete and named.
Wrinkle: textbook first-degree PD is allocatively efficient — the pushback is
distributional plus the elicitation arms race (consumers masking WTP =
false-signals recursion; note 4's deception-in-noise, consumer side). Academic
anchor: Dubé & Misra, *JPE* 131(1), Jan 2023, doi:10.1086/720793 — VERIFIED and on the
shelf in the trustworthy-firm collection; the majority-paid-less incidence
signature is confirmed.

## Addendum 3 (2026-07-30) — incidence of personalized pricing; the private-tax
## framing
Matthew asked whether efficiency implies a higher average price. No: efficiency
comes from more trade (excluded buyers served below the old uniform price;
high-WTP buyers pushed toward reservation values; average ambiguous —
Dubé-Misra's majority-paid-less is the signature). His tax framing is
structurally exact: a progressive tax on the good with privatized proceeds.
Opposition decomposes rationally: payers are affluent+vocal; no legitimacy of
the collector; dual-entitlement fairness norms against demand-based pricing
(Kahneman, Knetsch & Thaler, *AER* 76(4):728–741, Sept 1986 — VERIFIED from the typeset original; no DOI exists, Crossref carries only anthology reprints). Clincher:
university financial aid = accepted surveillance pricing (FAFSA as the
surveillance) — governance of the estimator and destination of surplus flip
acceptance, not the mechanism. Feeds the aggregation-point argument.

## Addendum 4 (2026-07-30) — financial aid's inflated baseline
Matthew: the aid mechanism is genuine personalized pricing, but its sticker
baseline was inflated by undischargeable government-backed student credit
(no default risk → no lender discipline → price ratchet), with aid
personalizing down from the inflated number. Verified: Lucca-Nadauld-Shen RFS
2018 (doi:10.1093/rfs/hhy069, credit→tuition passthrough) and Cellini-Goldin
AEJ:EP 2014 (for-profits capture aid via price). Bennett 1987 as the op-ed
conjecture. Upshots: legitimacy can be manufactured (halo over extraction —
warning for the fair-split version); the debt-forgiveness movement is the
Arrow-clause discharge in progress; corruption entered through a credit rule
two layers below the pricing layer.
