Matthew's distinction (2026-06-22), sharpening *why machine intelligence is the lever* (abstract inoculation, §5/§7/§8):

- Creating a standard (CXL, and standards generally) is itself an **assertion of the collective-action problem** — the industry coordinating because no one can go it alone.
- But a standard coordinates the **interface, not the competence**. CXL standardizes how memory connects; each vendor still implements it with its own hoarded optimization. It creates an interoperability commons, not an optimization/competence commons — it leaves the §2 realization gap untouched. Standards have been the field's answer for decades and never touch the hard layer.

**The ladder (ties to §7):**
- *Standard* — interface agreement only (CXL, PCIe, POSIX).
- *Open-source artifact* — frozen competence you inherit, but must still adapt with your own effort (LLVM, Linux).
- *Machine intelligence* — **generative** competence, applied per instance.

ML is the furthest point: the first thing that can **be** a copyable concentration of competence, not merely coordinate it (standard) or freeze it (repo). That is the inoculation stated as a mechanism — competence that copies without hoarding. ML is becoming **multiple** such concentrations (several frontier labs/models).

**Honest tempers (keep it from overclaiming):**
- "Beyond all centralized competence today" holds for *breadth* (no institution spans what a frontier model touches), NOT yet for *depth* in elite niches (§4 CUDA/HFT/codec). Keep the claim to breadth + trajectory; §8 already hedges depth.
- "Multiple concentrations" cuts in our favor twice: plurality answers §11's monoculture worry, and competition among concentrations checks the principal-agent risk.

**Paper ties:** deepens §5 (the real coordination failure is competence, not interface), §7 (the ladder), §8 (why ML is the lever), and mechanizes the abstract's inoculation. Reframes the CXL example [[cxl-unified-memory-idea]]: CXL's existence asserts the problem; its interface-only ceiling shows why ML is needed. Not yet in the paper.
