When writing or editing prose for Matthew (esp. *The Performance Commons*), use a confident, plain voice. Do NOT hedge defensively or preemptively apologize — e.g. avoid "the natural objection is overreach," "the honest other edge," throat-clearing that concedes weakness before anyone attacks. But do NOT overcorrect into self-congratulation or grandiosity either ("the reach now available to anyone who takes it," "it has never been wider" — too much). State the point plainly and concisely; let it stand without flourish. Concise > verbose.

**Why:** the paper's goal is to *be a challenge for people to see and reach their potential*, not to defend itself. Apologetic framing undercuts that purpose.

**How to apply:** Keep *substantive* honest caveats that the argument genuinely needs (e.g. the §8 velocity multiplier's rising defect rate; the n=1 status of the first experiment; conceptual/illustrative figure flags) — honesty is still required (see CLAUDE.md conventions). But frame limits as frontiers and invitations, not confessions: "these are open fronts, not finished products — and that is the invitation, not the disclaimer." Distinguish honest caveat (keep, state plainly) from defensive apology (cut). Related: [[[performance-commons-edit-program]]].
