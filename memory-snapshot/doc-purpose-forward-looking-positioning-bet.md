What *The Performance Commons* is (Matthew, 2026-06-22): **not a neutral academic proof but a forward-looking positioning document for forward-looking engineers** — about how Matthew is positioning himself across the stack and why he thinks it makes sense. (The abstract already frames the two questions for "forward looking engineers": how to design *assuming* the collective-action problem is solved.)

- **The load-bearing bet:** machine intelligence will gain **depth**, not just breadth. Today it has breadth (spans more than any institution) but not uniform depth in elite niches (§4 CUDA/HFT/codec). Matthew is betting depth arrives soon.
- **Why the near-term move is modest:** because depth isn't here yet, the low-hanging fruit is the §12 FPGA profile→reconfigure experiment, not "replace all computers now." Start at n=1.
- **Register implication:** the honest present state (breadth-only, n=1, nothing finished) is the *starting line of a bet*, not a weakness to apologize for. Confidence comes from the trajectory + reasoning stated plainly — not from pretending depth is settled. See [[writing-voice-challenge-not-apology]].
- **Candidate edit:** state the depth-bet explicitly as the central contingency — a §11 limit or a hinge sentence near §8/§10. §8 currently only *hedges* depth; a forward-looking doc is more credible when it names the bet and what would falsify it. **PARTIALLY DONE (2026-07-19):** §5's fairness-to-the-herd paragraph now names "another part of this paper's bet: that machine intelligence raises the ambient competence… far enough that designing the right thing becomes cheaper than reusing the last one" — the competence/depth bet is in the text; a §11 falsifiability framing remains optional.
